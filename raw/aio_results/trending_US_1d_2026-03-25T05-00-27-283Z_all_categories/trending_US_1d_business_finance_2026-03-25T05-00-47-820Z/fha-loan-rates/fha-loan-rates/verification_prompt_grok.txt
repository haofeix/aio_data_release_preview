For the most part, FHA loan interest rates are subject to the same influences as conventional loan rates. Credit score: While FHA loans allow for lower credit scores, borrowers with higher credit scores typically qualify for better interest rates since they represent less risk to lenders. Debt-to-income ratio: A higher debt-to-income ratio (DTI), or how much of your monthly income goes toward paying debts, can indicate financial overextension; this may lead lenders to offer higher interest rates to offset the increased risk. Loan amount: Larger loan amounts may come with slightly higher rates because they increase the lender's exposure if the borrower defaults. Repayment term: Shorter terms tend to have lower interest rates than longer terms since lenders are repaid more quickly and face less risk of default. Loan purpose: Whether you're buying a home, refinancing or taking cash out of your home, the type of loan you choose can impact your rate. FHA Loan Requirements To qualify for an FHA loan, you'll need to meet financial, credit and property-related criteria set by both the FHA and your lender. While some requirements may vary by lender, here are the most common ones to expect: Credit score: FHA loans require a minimum credit score of 580 to qualify for the minimum down payment option. However, with a down payment of 10% or more, you may be eligible with a score as low as 500. DTI: Most lenders prefer a DTI below 43%, though higher ratios may be allowed with strong compensating factors like additional cash reserves or high credit scores. The maximum DTI for an FHA loan is 57%. Income and employment: Lenders like to see a history of steady employment, so you may need to provide at least two years' worth of income through tax returns, recent pay stubs, W-2s, bank statements and other financial documents.
