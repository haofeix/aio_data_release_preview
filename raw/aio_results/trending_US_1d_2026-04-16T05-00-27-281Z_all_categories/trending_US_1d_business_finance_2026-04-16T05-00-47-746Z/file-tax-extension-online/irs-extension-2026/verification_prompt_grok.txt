If you haven't filed your tax return yet and don't have the time or just don't feel like doing it by the deadline, you can file an extension for free online.The IRS tax filing deadline for most Americans is April 15, and failing to file a tax return by the deadline can result in a late filing fee. Millions of people each year request and receive a filing extension, which eliminates the late filing penalties, although, if a person owes taxes they're still required to pay them by the deadline or they could get a financial penalty. How to File an IRS Tax Return Extension Filing a federal tax extension is straightforward and automatic if it’s done by the deadline. The IRS offers several ways to do it, all centered on Form 4868, the Application for Automatic Extension of Time to File U.S. Individual Income Tax Return. You can request an extension in one of three main ways:File Form 4868 electronically: Most tax software programs and IRS‑authorized e‑file providers allow taxpayers to submit Form 4868 online. This is the fastest option and provides confirmation that the IRS accepted the request. Use IRS Free File or Free Fillable Forms: The IRS allows anyone, regardless of income, to file an extension for free using its Free File system or Free Fillable Forms. Make an electronic payment and mark it as an extension: Taxpayers who make a payment through IRS Direct Pay, debit or credit card, or a digital wallet can indicate that the payment is for an extension. In that case, a separate Form 4868 is not required. File Form 4868 by mail: the extension request form can be sent by mail to the relevant IRS office depending on where individuals list. Addresses are listed in the form. Internal Revenue Service is engraved over the entrance to the headquarters building on March 24, 2026, in Washington. | J. David Ake/Getty ImagesForm 4868 automatically grants a six‑month extension, moving the filing deadline to October 15, as long as it is submitted by April 15.It’s important to note that the extension applies only to filing the return, not to paying taxes owed.
