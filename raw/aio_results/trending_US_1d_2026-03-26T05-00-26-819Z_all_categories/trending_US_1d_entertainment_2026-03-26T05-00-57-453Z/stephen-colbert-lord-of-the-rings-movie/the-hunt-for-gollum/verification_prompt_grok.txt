The Lord of the Rings: The Hunt for Gollum is the working title of an upcoming film produced by Warner Bros. under its New Line Cinema studio and distribution subsidiary. It will be directed by Andy Serkis with Peter Jackson, Fran Walsh, and Philippa Boyens as producers,[2] and was set to be released in 2026,[3] but slipped to December 2027 according to Serkis.[4] In early May 2025, its premiere date was set at December 17, 2027.[1] Contents 1 Cast 2 Crew 3 Plot 4 Development 4.1 Casting 5 Notes 6 Gallery 7 References 8 External links Cast[] Andy Serkis as Gollum[5] Ian McKellen (semi-official)[6][7][8] as Gandalf[9] Elijah Wood (unofficial,[10] despite source claim)[11] as Frodo Baggins[9] Crew[] Director: Andy Serkis Producers: Peter Jackson, Fran Walsh, Philippa Boyens, Pamela Abdy, Jonathan Cavendish, Mike De Luca, Ken Kamins, Andy Serkis, and David Zaslav Writers: Fran WalshPhilippa BoyensPhoebe GittinsArty Papageorgiou Plot[] The film depicts the titular "hunt for Gollum," prior to the events of The Fellowship of the Ring.[12] During this period, Aragorn, Gandalf, and Sauron are all looking for Gollum. The story is told from Gollum's perspective.[13] Development[] Discussions for another Lord of the Rings film began sometime after the release of The Hobbit: The Battle of the Five Armies.[14] Serkis was brought onboard around November 2023, as Warner Bros. wanted to rejuvenate the Middle-earth IP, starting with a Gollum film.[citation needed] Serkis was asked to direct with Peter Jackson returning to produce.[2][15] It was reported in May 2024, that the film was in early script development involving Walsh and Boyens, along with Phoebe Gittins and Arty Papageorgiou.[2] As of December 2024, Philippa Boyens is working on a script or treatment.[16] Filming is expected to begin early or mid 2026,[17] but likely May 2026 according to Ian McKellen.[9] Andy Serkis and key members of the crew arrived in New Zealand around September 10, 2025.[18] Elijah Wood seems to have read preliminary scripts and has responded extremely positively, while strongly implying his return.[19][11] While he has been seen in New Zealand in late 2025,[20] his most recent film role in Bookworm (2024) was filmed in New Zealand and he could be there just for some related followup.[21] Casting[] The only certain casting is Andy Serkis as Gollum and possibly as Smeagol. At the 2025 For the Love of Fantasy[22] in London, Ian McKellen largely confirmed that the Gandalf and Frodo characters would be appearing in the film and hinted it could be the return for himself and Elijah Wood, but did not confirm it.[9] Notes[] As of May, 2024, The Hunt for Gollum is one of two The Lord of the Rings films intended to be produced.[14] The second is another live-action movie that is currently unnamed.[23] Apparently Ian McKellen may have prompted a rumor that The Hunt for Gollum would be two films.[24] Ian McKellen stated in June, 2024, that he had heard Gandalf would appear in the film, but that he had not been approached to reprise the role.[25] In September of the same year, he confirmed that he had been approached to reprise it for both Hunt and other films that would involve Gandalf.[26] In the same month, Serkis hinted that there would be characters returning to the film, stating "there may be characters that we recognize that might be coming back. I’m not going to say who."[12] In September 2024, it was confirmed that Orlando Bloom had spoken to Serkis concerning reprising his role as Legolas. The use of AI to make the character appear younger was discussed.[27] On December 4, 2024, it was reported from an interview excerpt that Philippa Boyens expressed the development team's hope that Viggo Mortensen would return to play Aragorn.[28] She also said she, Andy Serkis, and Peter Jackson had all spoken to him, however his return has not been confirmed as of March 2025. In late 2025, several rumors circulated that the role of Aragorn would be re-cast and Viggo Mortensen would not return.[29][30] Although unconfirmed, Mortensen has definitely not spoken of a return and reports of the producers looking for an actor to potentially replace him are probably true. Based on the appendices of J. R. R. Tolkien's 1954–55 book The Lord of the Rings, the film is set in Middle-earth, when the wizard Gandalf the Grey fears that Gollum may reveal information about the One Ring to Sauron. Gandalf sends the ranger Aragorn on a quest to find Gollum.[a] Filming took place in North Wales, Epping Forest, and Hampstead Heath. The film was shot in high definition video, with a budget of £3,000 (equivalent to £4,787 in 2025[2] or US$5,000, equivalent to $7,503 in 2025[3]). The production is completely unofficial and unauthorized, though Bouchard said he had "reached an understanding" with Tolkien Enterprises in 2009. The Hunt for Gollum debuted at the Sci-Fi-London film festival and on the Internet, free to view, on 3 May 2009. By 20 October 2009, it had been viewed by 5 million people. Viewings had risen to over 14 million by 2025. The film is set during the timespan of The Fellowship of the Ring. It takes place 17 years after Bilbo Baggins's 111th birthday party and just before Frodo Baggins leaves the Shire for Rivendell (an interval not outlined in Jackson's film of that story). The wizard Gandalf fears that Gollum may reveal information about the One Ring to the Dark Lord Sauron, and sends the Ranger Aragorn, heir of Isildur, on a quest to find him. The story opens with a brief prologue about the ring's disappearance before cutting to Aragorn and Gandalf at an inn (presumably the Prancing Pony) in Bree. Gandalf explains his concerns about Gollum's knowledge of the Ring falling into enemy hands, and asks Aragorn to find him with his tracking skills. After a fruitless search, Aragorn crosses paths with Arithir, a fellow Ranger of the North, who reports rumours about a creature that steals fish from open windows in villages; the film cuts to a scene of Gollum doing just that, and eating his ill-gotten gains atop the roof. Aragorn encounters and kills a pair of orc scouts in the forest. He soon locates Gollum by a fish pond and captures him in a snare trap. Having secured the whining and protesting Gollum inside a sack, Aragorn sets out for Mirkwood. On the way, he spots a Ringwraith in the woods, but avoids it. Later that day, he is attacked by an orc squad and defeats them, but is hit by a poison dart which takes effect after he kills his last attacker. He collapses beside a patch of Athelas flowers and has a vision of Arwen in Rivendell. Aragorn regains consciousness at dusk and discovers that Gollum has escaped. He seeks him well into the night, and finds him hiding in a tree. Gollum fearfully explains that a Ringwraith is coming; seconds later, a Ringwraith appears and attacks Aragorn. After a short duel, the Ringwraith flees from a bright light created by the Elves of Mirkwood, who recapture Gollum and guide Aragorn back to their fortress. The scene cuts to Gandalf emerging from Mirkwood's dungeons after interrogating Gollum. Gandalf tells Aragorn that Gollum knows of Bilbo Baggins and the Shire, and explains that he must now go there to warn Frodo. Aragorn suggests sending Frodo to meet him in Bree, and Gandalf readily agrees. The film ends with Gollum speaking to himself in the dungeon, where he vows to kill "Bagginses" and reclaim his "Precious". Adrian Webster as Aragorn II Elessar, a Dúnedain ranger, the descendant of Isildur, and heir to Gondor's throne. He travels on a journey to find Gollum. Media coverage has noted Webster's resemblance to Viggo Mortensen, who played Aragorn in The Lord of the Rings film trilogy.[4][5][6] Arin Alldridge as Arithir, a ranger and distant kinsman. He is an original character.[7] Patrick O'Connor as Gandalf the Grey,[7] an Istari wizard. As with Webster and Mortensen, coverage has noted O'Connor's resemblance to Ian McKellen, who played Gandalf in The Lord of the Rings film trilogy.[5][6] Rita Ramnani as Arwen,[7] an elven daughter of Elrond, lord of Rivendell, and Aragorn's true love. Gareth Brough voices Gollum,[7] a wretched hobbit-like[8] creature whose mind was poisoned over centuries by the Ring; and Goblok, an orc chieftain, an original character. Jason Perino also voices Gollum, while Mathew Cunningham, Christopher Dinglias and Francesco San Juan play the character's body. Max Bracey as a Mirkwood Elf. The screenplay was written by Chris Bouchard with Stuart Price and Lewis Albrow.[9] Rickety Shack Films and Independent Online Cinema handled the production.[9] It was unclear whether the production violated the rights held by the Tolkien Estate and New Line Cinema.
