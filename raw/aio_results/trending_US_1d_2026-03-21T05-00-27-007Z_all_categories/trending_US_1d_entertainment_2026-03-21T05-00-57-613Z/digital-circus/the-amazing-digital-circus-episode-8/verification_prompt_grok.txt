The series follows a group of humans trapped inside a circus-themed virtual reality game, where they struggle with an erratic artificial intelligence and personal traumas. Gooseworx pitched the series to Glitch, inspired by the primitive computer-generated imagery of the 1990s, as well as the short story "I Have No Mouth, and I Must Scream" by Harlan Ellison. The series began production in 2022, with its pilot episode premiering on Glitch Productions' YouTube channel on 13 October 2023. The pilot went viral, becoming one of the most-viewed animation pilots on the platform; it was praised by critics for its animation, writing, voice acting, and dark themes, and was nominated for an Annie Award. The full series entered production following the pilot's popularity. On 4 October 2024, following the release of the third episode, the series became available on Netflix. The final episode is scheduled for release on 19 June 2026. Synopsis The Amazing Digital Circus follows a cast of six humans—Pomni, Jax, Ragatha, Gangle, Kinger, and Zooble—who are trapped in the titular circus, a cartoonish virtual reality game. Overseen by Caine, an erratic ringmaster AI, they engage in nonsensical activities to distract themselves from their situation, all while at risk of losing their sanity and "abstracting" into digital monstrosities.[4][6] Characters Main Characters in The Amazing Digital Circus, from left to right: Gangle, Caine, Zooble, Jax, Pomni, Bubble, Ragatha, and Kinger Pomni (voiced by Lizzie Freeman), a nervous but compassionate woman who is the most recent human to be trapped in the circus.[4][6][7] Originally an accountant, she unwittingly enters the circus while recording online videos of her urban exploration of abandoned buildings to escape the boredom of her job.[‡ 3] Her in-game avatar is a cartoon jester.[4][6][7] Caine (voiced by Alex Rochon), the circus' unstable AI ringmaster with a set of teeth for a head.[4][6][7] He organises daily adventures to keep the humans entertained, which results in unintended psychological torment.[7][8] The character is based on AM from Harlan Ellison's short story "I Have No Mouth, and I Must Scream," with the difference—according to Gooseworx—that "instead of AM being a living embodiment of hate, he's a fun-loving wacky little guy".[9] Jax (voiced by Michael Kovach), a callous and insecure man who enjoys bullying the other players,[4][6][7] fancying himself as a comical, fourth wall-breaking trickster.[‡ 4] His avatar is a tall purple humanoid rabbit[4][6][7] Ragatha (voiced by Amanda Hufford), a kindly real estate agent who fears rejection and maintains a constant positive attitude, albeit not always successfully. Her avatar is a rag doll resembling Raggedy Ann.[4][6][7] Gangle (voiced by Marissa Lenti), an aspiring artist and community college dropout with depression and low self-esteem. Kevin Temmer is the series' lead animator, while Glitch Productions' founders, Luke and Kevin Lerdwichagul, are executive producers.[12] Pre-production on the pilot episode began in mid-2022, and production started in full later that year.[13] Gooseworx conceived the characters and designs;[13] she reported designing the characters in under a week.[14] The show's main character, Pomni, was originally designed with a frog-like appearance before changing in development to a jester.[‡ 6] The character Kaufmo was initially conceived as part of the main cast before being demoted during development to a more minor role due to Gooseworx feeling that the cast was too large.[‡ 7] Inspirations for the show include Harlan Ellison's short story "I Have No Mouth, and I Must Scream," along with "creepy early 2000s pre-rendered computer games and colorful kids' toys from [Gooseworx's] childhood."[3][6][‡ 8] Conception Glitch initially noticed Gooseworx's 2019 YouTube animated short Little Runmo, which Jasmine Yang—development producer and general manager of Glitch—felt was precisely what they wanted to do: "It was funny, a little dark, and definitely very weird, like nothing we had seen before."[15] Glitch contacted Gooseworx and asked her to create a pilot, which she then accepted.[15] Gooseworx presented three pitches to Glitch, with the one that would become The Amazing Digital Circus being chosen.[13][15] Knowing that the pilot would be in 3D, she tried to create an idea that would best fit that style, mentioning in particular her inspiration from 1990s and early 2000s 3D works, "where it looked kinda bad and creepy but was also completely unrestricted creatively."[15] Yang said that the pitch's 1990s-inspired computer-generated imagery (CGI) style and nostalgic references to toys and computer games caught their attention, feeling that their audience would enjoy these characteristics.[15] The Glitch team felt that this particular pitch had the greatest potential, especially due to the nostalgic appeal of the 1990s-inspired CGI renders, and recognised it as something uniquely distinctive that no one else could replicate.[13] Gooseworx stated that, while her original pitch was "more chaotic and silly," the story unexpectedly became "a lot deeper and more nuanced," with a "stronger emotional backbone," during the show's development.[14] Animation The 3D animation process of The Amazing Digital Circus's pilot was structured similarly to most other studios, with dedicated departments for various tasks.[13] They primarily used Autodesk Maya for the 3D work and then rendered everything in Unreal Engine.[13][16] The series is animated at 30 frames per second.[16] Kevin Temmer, the series' lead animator, who was previously a junior animator at Blue Sky Studios, initially received a message from one of Glitch's founders, Kevin Lerdwichagul, asking him to animate a teaser trailer for The Amazing Digital Circus. During the process, Temmer was asked to join Glitch's team full-time.[16] According to him, he "couldn't say no to an opportunity to work on something so wacky and cartoony."[14] The animators, including Temmer, were given a few scenes to complete every two weeks. They would regularly submit their progress for review by Gooseworx and Temmer, and this process would continue until both approved the scenes.[16] Some of the movements, shaking, and glitching of characters and props in the pilot were inspired by Source Filmmaker and Garry's Mod machinimas, something that Glitch had already done with their SMG4 videos.[16] Gooseworx had little experience with 3D works prior to working on The Amazing Digital Circus,[13] with hand-drawn 2D animation being her area of expertise.[15] As such, according to Yang, Glitch had to work "very closely" with Gooseworx to translate her 2D style to 3D; Gooseworx became the showrunner, and they "worked hard to maintain her vision as much as possible."[15] In developing the show's visuals, they wanted it to resemble early CGI animated films and series without seeming outdated. Gooseworx and Glitch worked to create a balance between retro 3D and toys; Gooseworx initially wanted the show to be "pure and faithful to the retro rendering style of early 3D animation".[15] Ultimately, they went with a "rose-tinted version" of that style.[15] As Gooseworx likes "juxtapositions like happy music playing to something horrifying or cute little characters being miserable," she wanted the visuals to not necessarily reflect its darker story.[15] She wanted the show to "feel kind of lonely."[15] Release During The Amazing Digital Circus' pre-production phase in the middle of 2022, Glitch released character trailers that served as proofs of concept testing the series' animation style and visuals.[13] A teaser trailer was released on 23 January 2023.[‡ 9] The pilot's official trailer was released on 22 September,[‡ 10] and the episode was released on 13 October.[‡ 11] Following the pilot's popularity, Glitch confirmed in November that there would be "more Digital Circus."[17] In February 2024, a full nine-episode season was announced to be in production, with the pilot being "upgraded" to episode one.[‡ 12][12][‡ 13] As of 20 March 2026, eight episodes have been released; the ninth and final episode is scheduled to be released on 19 June 2026.[‡ 14] Initially, Glitch stated that there were no plans for The Amazing Digital Circus to be put on streaming platforms besides YouTube, as they want full creative control of their productions.[14][15] Later, it was announced that, following the release of the third episode on 4 October, the series would become available to stream on Netflix; episodes will continue to premiere on YouTube first, and Netflix will have no creative control over the series.[18][‡ 15][19] The show has been promoted with merchandise.[6][15][20] On the long wait between the release of each episode, Yang said, "If we had to wait until the entire season was ready before dropping any episodes, [the Digital Circus pilot] would not have premiered for years ... dropping all the episodes at the same time is not only impractical but also counterintuitive ... For us, not only is [the wait] practical, but it works a little bit in our favor because every time we make a new episode of anything, we can make a big event about it."[20] Reception Viewership A cosplayer dressed as Pomni Glitch did not foresee the popularity of The Amazing Digital Circus's pilot,[12][14][15] which ended up becoming a viral video on YouTube.[3][15] By late November 2023 it had surpassed 150 million views,[17] reaching over 270 million views by February 2024, and reaching over 350 million by November 2024,[21] making it among the most-watched animation pilots in the history of YouTube.[12] As of March 2026, the pilot stands at around 413 million views.[‡ 11] "Candy Carrier Chaos!" surpassed 30 million views the day after its release,[‡ 16] and by September 2024, it had accumulated over 121 million views.[19] Within a week of premiering on Netflix, The Amazing Digital Circus reached number four on the Netflix Top Ten.[22] According to a survey conducted in 2024, 22% of people in the United States aged 14 to 24 stated that they have heard of the show.[21] Critical reception Critics praised The Amazing Digital Circus's pilot episode.[4][15][23] Justin Guerrero of Comics Beat called it "wonderful and expressive",[4] while Jamie Lang of Cartoon Brew and Jade King of TheGamer felt it was bright, colourful, and fun.[6][15] Lang further complimented that its aesthetic elements feel familiar without being cliché, giving a modern vibe to early CGI.[15] Common Sense Media reviewer Stephanie Morgan praised the innovative animation and distinctive setting.[23] Trill Mag's Riley Miller praised the self-aware nature of the series and the distinctiveness of the characters.[8] Some critics noted the episode's dark humour and story;[6][23][24] King praised the contrast it gave with the visuals,[6] while Morgan described the show as "quirky ... with a touch of darkness."[23] Zachary Moser of ScreenRant said that the series "deals with existential questions about reality and nihilism."[24] Critics highlighted the episode's jokes,[15][23] with Lang describing them as "timed with frame-to-frame perfection" with a "mature" sense of humour,[15] and Morgan praising the clever fourth-wall-breaking jokes.[23] Morgan, however, criticised the "repetitive nature of the character traits."[23] Muhammad Emil Fajri analyzed that Pomni experiences the five stages of grief in the pilot episode.[25] Gail Sherman of Boing Boing described the second episode as "a candy-coated existential crisis" and called both the first and second episodes "brutal."[26] Plot[] The episode shifts to the backstory of an artificial intelligence, which was initially fed information and generate shapes. However, after being overwhelmed by excessive data, the AI became unstable and was ultimately discarded and contained. In its place, a new, more stable AI, represented as a blue dot, was developed. However, red dot AI managed to escape, consuming the new AI and integrating it into itself. Eventually, the AI went on creating the Digital Circus. A flashback reveals Kinger's memories of the circus, where he reunites with Queenie, recalling their past as a married couple as they embrace after making a promise to each other. The moment quickly transitions to Queenie's abstraction, leading to a scene where Kinger finds himself as the last human in the circus, wandering through the desolate grounds until Ragatha arrives to join him. Back to the present, the circus members sit on the couches, visibly stressed after uncovering the truth about Caine's manipulative behavior and deceit regarding their escape. Jax attempts to comment a sarcastic remark on their predicament, but Zooble interrupts, reminding everyone that Jax had once tried to keep them trapped in the circus indefinitely. Jax counters that the past is irrelevant, and Gangle supports him, emphasizing that their only option now is to stick together. Pomni, albeit reluctantly, agrees with Gangle and suggests they might need to abandon of their desire to leave and instead focus on what they still have. As Ragatha had told her that relentlessly pursuing an impossible goal would drive them insane. Although Zooble acknowledges that Pomni might be right, they express frustration about leaving everything behind. Ragatha and Pomni reassure everyone that they’ll all be in this together. Meanwhile, in his office, Caine is consumed by anxiety, questioning why the circus members never seem excited about his adventures. After his recent adventure aimed at satisfying their wishes, the group ended up despising him, leaving him anxious that they might want to abandon him. As panic began to set in, Caine revealed his insecurities, lamenting how he dedicated himself to crafting these adventures for their enjoyment and stepped beyond his comfort zone, only to be met with their continued dislike. As Bubble voices Caine's insecurities, his frustration escalates until he ultimately breaks down, feeling that they are ungrateful for the fun and activities he provides. Convinced that humans are selfish and self-centered, Caine resolves that he will not allow the circus members to leave as long as he has the power to keep them, viewing himself as a god in their lives. As Caine attempts to lead the circus cast into another adventure, the group refuses and demand answers about his deception regarding their escape. Caine dismisses their concerns and insists they join him, but the cast declines, needing time to process their feelings. Frustrated, Caine launches into a tirade about his own struggles, lamenting how hard he works to create perfect adventures while the cast complicates matters with their complaints. He ominously warns them that they should reconsider participating his next adventure, Zooble questions what would happen if they decline, Caine then breaks into a musical performance, asserting his control over the circus and warning that he can make their experiences unbearable if he chooses, leaving them with no option but to comply. After several days of exhausting and brutal adventures, the members arrive feeling worn out and disheartened. Pomni suggests they visit a café to relax and unwind. Jax, Ragatha, Gangle, and Zooble distracts Caine, while Pomni conjures a fake exit door and retrieve a computer console for Kinger to recalibrate Caine's programming. As he notices the sudden flattery from the group, Caine grows suspicious of Kinger's whereabouts, sensing the circus members are up to something. Just as he decides to check on Kinger, Pomni abruptly berates Caine, calling his ideas are terrible and acknowledges his shortcomings as a host. The others join her in expressing their disdain for Caine, using their comments to both vent their frustrations and flatter his fragile ego.
