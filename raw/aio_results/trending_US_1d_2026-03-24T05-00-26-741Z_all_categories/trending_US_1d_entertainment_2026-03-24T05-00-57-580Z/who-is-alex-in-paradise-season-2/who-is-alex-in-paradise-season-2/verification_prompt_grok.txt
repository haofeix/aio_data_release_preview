Paradise Season 2, Episode 7 Ending Explained: “The Final Countdown” Recap Things truly started to ramp up in Paradise Season 2’s penultimate episode when Link and Sinatra finally came face to face, Sinatra handed over his requested apple pie, and the two hashed things out in a dramatic Air Force One meeting. Please forget about this and move on.” In response, Link doubled down. Hard. “Lady you’re not quite getting it, we’re coming in. I will find Alex, and we will end this,” he said. Photo: Disney/Ser Baffo Sinatra thinks that Link is her late son, Dylan, who died of a mystery illness years before the the bunker was built. Earlier in the episode, Sinatra’s husband casually mentioned “Dylan would have been 26 this year,” the same age Link told Sinatra he was. When hacking Sinatra’s computer earlier, her daughter also noted the password was May 16, Dylan’s birthday. Link has the same name and birthday as Sinatra’s late son Dylan, which leads her to believe he is her son. When she goes back to her husband after the encounter, she even tells him, “It worked! I think it worked…I think [I’m OK]. I can’t explain it, but I think Dylan is, too.” Confused? Nicholson shed some light on the big twist, which she recalled finding out early in the season. “I was like, ‘Wait, how does that work?’ Physics! Quantum physics, guys! Get on board with it!” she told DECIDER. “I found it very moving. I obviously don’t know quantum physics, so I had to have faith in the writers, who did all this research. But I found it very moving, even just for Sinatra to imagine that this is her son. We don’t we don’t know, exactly. But the idea of it, and the peace it brought her, and the hopefulness was really beautiful.” As for Sinatra and Link’s nose bleeds, as noted in a prior article, they appear to happen when there’s a reverberation or change in time (more on this in a moment). They could also be a sign of temporal displacement, a time travel-y term that signifies when someone is unstuck in time, which we saw on Lost! So how is Link Sinatra’s son Dylan if Sinatra’s son died? Great question! While we don’t fully know the answer, it’s definitely related to Alex! Photo: Disney/Ser Baffo Who Is Alex In Paradise? Or Rather, What Is Alex? Since Season 2’s premiere it’s been clear that some sort of time manipulation is taking place in Paradise, and it all ties back to the mysterious Alex. In a flashback this season, scientist Dr. Louge (Geoffrey Arend) told Sinatra, “There’s only one thing that can fix this, and it’s the one thing even you can’t buy…time.” And when discussing Dylan’s death in a Season 1 flashback, Sinatra said, “Everybody said time would help. But it’s making it worse because it’s taking me further away from when he was here.” Even before the bunker, the concept of time and Dylan’s death weighed heavily on Sinatra and fueled her actions — both seen and secretive. “[Dylan’s] loss is is basically why Paradise exists. Her need to protect her remaining family has been at the top of her list ever since then. Everything is driven with that as the end goal,” Nicholson said. “I found that whole speech so moving from the first season, so to be able to return to that now and have an understanding that it never was just about the bunker, she did have a larger plan in mind from the very beginning, having little clues as to that plan working was really fun to explore.” Photo: Disney/Ser Baffo So who or what is Alex? And why is Link trying to “kill” Alex? Episode 3 revealed that Link’s former business partner/mentor, professor Henry Miller (Patrick Fischler), had a company called Vestige Quantum with technology so valuable to Sinatra that she had Billy kill him for it. Miller also had a wife named Alex, who suffered from Huntington’s Disease, and who he seemingly killed before Billy killed him. Because of Miller’s wife, viewers assumed Sinatra’s side project “Alex” was a person, but it’s increasingly likely that Alex is a codename for the advanced technology Sinatra’s using to manipulate time. I’m no quantum physicist, but quantum entanglement is the idea that “When two particles, such as a pair of photons or electrons, become entangled, they remain connected even when separated by vast distances.” And superposition deals with things existing in multiple states at once until observed. Sinatra’s side project is driving the Lost-style time travel/multiverse/causality loop-type twist. So it’s possible that multiple timelines exist at once, or perhaps she’s simply trying to go back in time and alter certain fates. Episode 6, which showed “Alex” sending warning messages about Jane’s upcoming birth to a man in 1997, suggested that Sinatra is using Alex to send messages back in time in hopes of re-shaping or tweaking the future. And Link likely knows the reverberations from those efforts can have dire consequences, so he’s trying to stop her. As a result of her work with Alex, it seems Sinatra believes her son Dylan survived — either in another timeline or as a result of alterations she made. But whether she got her quantum physics on to prevent Dylan’s death, stop the super volcano from erupting, both, or some secret fourth thing has yet to be revealed. At the end of Episode 7, Sinatra takes a secret underground tram and walks down the same hallway we saw in Xavier’s vision where he’s following Link (another example of time being shuffled). When she arrives at the restricted area that holds her secret project, she puts on a blue coat, opens the door, looks up, and says “Hi, Alex” as a warm orange glow shines down on her. At the time of writing this post, critics have only seen seven episodes of Season 2, so I have no idea what Paradise has in store either. Like viewers, I’ll be hypothesizing, brushing up on quantum physics, and counting down the days until the finale!
