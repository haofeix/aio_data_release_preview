He slashed .267/.406/.613, from 106 games, his second consecutive year with an OPS over 1.000 and his third overall.[196] He had 98 hits, 16 doubles, 75 RBI, 88 walks, 9 intentional walks, and 3 stolen bases (caught stealing once).[196] The Yankees finished the regular season in fourth place in the AL East and missed the playoffs for the first time since 2016.[197] Judge received the Roberto Clemente Award before Game 3 of the World Series. The award acknowledged his exemplary character and charity work promoting responsible youth and civic citizenship in New York and California through his ALL RISE Foundation.[198][199] Judge was also selected to the All-MLB Team for the third successive year, this time to the second team.[200] He finished 15th in AL MVP voting.[201] As captain, Judge appeared to take a more hands-on approach towards the Yankees organization, having conversations with Steinbrenner about the team direction and potential changes following the Yankees missing the postseason.[202] 2024: Second MVP, AL pennant Judge in 2024 Following an injury-interrupted spring training, Judge started the 2024 season slowly, hitting .180 through the first three weeks of April.[203][204][205] Judge primarily played at center field early in the season but also made his first major league appearance in left field when Alex Verdugo was on paternity leave.[206][207] By late April, Judge appeared more comfortable and improved his performance at the plate, collecting eight hits and three home runs along with six walks in the final week of the month.[204] Judge was selected as the AL Player of the Week for the week of May 19, after going 10-for-20 and slugging 1.200 with three home runs and five doubles in this period.[154][208] Judge was also selected as the AL Player of the Month for May, having hit 14 home runs and 12 doubles in the month, with 27 RBI, slashing .361/.479/.918.[204][209] This marked Judge's seventh AL Player of the Month selection, and his third consecutive May honor.[209] Judge also completed a homecoming of sorts, playing his first series at Oracle Park against the San Francisco Giants, where he went 6-for-10 with three home runs and six RBI as the Yankees swept the series.[210] By the end of May, Judge improved his season slash line to .277/.405/.643, with an OPS of 1.048.[211] For the week ending June 9, Judge received his second AL Player of the Week award for 2024.[154] In the six-game homestand split against the Minnesota Twins and Los Angeles Dodgers, Judge slashed .500/.630/1.200 with three home runs and 12 RBI.[212] Following a 3-for-4 game against the Dodgers on June 9, Judge raised his season batting average to over .300 for the first time.[210] At end of June, Judge led the majors in home runs (31), RBI (82), and OPS (1.144), and he was second in the AL in batting average (.316) among qualified hitters.[213][214] Judge won the AL Player of the Month Award again in June, for the second consecutive month, slashing .409/.514/.864, with 11 home runs and 37 RBI for the month.[148][215] On June 27, Judge was announced as a starting outfielder for the AL in the All-Star Game, having received the most votes.[216] This was the second time in three seasons that he received the most All-Star votes.[217] On August 14, batting against Chad Kuhl of the Chicago White Sox, Judge hit his 300th career home run in the Yankees' 10–2 victory. He became the fastest player to ever hit 300 home runs, taking him 955 games and 3,431 at-bats, surpassing both Ralph Kiner's record by 132 games and Babe Ruth's by 400 at-bats.[218][219] He became the second hitter to record his 300th home run prior to his 1,000th hit.[220] He hit his 50th and 51st home runs of the season on August 25, becoming the fifth player in MLB history with three-plus seasons of 50 or more home runs.[221] Judge was named the AL Player of the Week for the week of August 19–25, following a strong homestand against the Cleveland Guardians and Colorado Rockies.[222] On August 26, Judge collected his 1,000th career hit, a line-drive single to right field against José Ferrer at Nationals Park.[223] Judge received the August AL Player of the Month Award, his third monthly award of the season, after slashing .389/.530/.856.[224] On September 13, Judge hit a go-ahead grand slam at Yankee Stadium against the Boston Red Sox.[225] This ended a 16-game homerless streak, the longest of his major league career.[226] Judge ended the regular season slashing .322/.458/.701, with 180 hits, 58 home runs, one triple, 36 doubles, 144 RBI, 133 walks, 20 intentional walks and 10 stolen bases from 155 games.[210] The Yankees won the ALCS against the Cleveland Guardians in five games in which Judge hit two home runs and notched five RBIs. In his first World Series, Judge went 1-for-12 with seven strikeouts in the first three games of the series as the Yankees fell into a 3–0 series deficit.[227][228] In Game 5, Judge hit his first World Series home run as the Yankees jumped out to a 5–0 lead, but a series of defensive mistakes, including Judge's first error of the year, allowed the lead to slip away as the Yankees lost the game 7–6, and thus the series in five games.[229] After the season, Judge won the AL MVP Award. He became the second Yankee player to win the award unanimously, following Mickey Mantle in 1956.[230] 2025: Third MVP Judge in 2025 On March 29, Judge hit three home runs and a double with eight RBIs against the Milwaukee Brewers, his third career three-homer game.[231] On March 31, Judge was named the AL Player of the Week for the opening week of the season after going 6-for-11 and slugging 1.818 with four home runs and 11 RBIs.[232] On April 3, Judge hit his 500th career extra-base hit with a first-inning, three-run home run off the Arizona Diamondbacks' Merrill Kelly. Judge became the third-fastest Yankee to reach the milestone in terms of games played, having done so in his 999th game.[233] In the same game, Judge became the first player in MLB history with five home runs and 15 RBIs in his team's first six games of the season. On April 4 against the Pittsburgh Pirates, playing in his 1,000th game for the Yankees, Judge registered his 321st home run, equaling Babe Ruth's record for home runs in his first 1,000 Yankees games.[234] By the end of April, Judge was slashing .427/.521/.761 with 10 home runs and 32 RBI.[235] Judge won with the AL Player of the Month Award for April, the tenth of his career.[236] On May 30 against the Los Angeles Dodgers, Judge and Shohei Ohtani became the first reigning MVPs ever to each hit a home run in the first inning of the same game.[237] Judge was named the AL Player of the Month again in May, after slashing .364/.453/.798, with 11 home runs on the month. His 11th career monthly award is the most in AL history, surpassing Alex Rodriguez.[238][236] On June 26, Judge was announced as a starting outfielder for the AL in the All-Star Game, having received the most votes for the third time in four seasons and the second straight year.[239][240] On July 12, Judge hit his 350th career home run against the Chicago Cubs; he accomplished this in 1,088 games, breaking the record of 1,280 games by Mark McGwire.[241] On July 27, Judge was placed on the 10-day injury list with a flexor strain in his right elbow, following discomfort while throwing from the outfield against the Toronto Blue Jays.[242] He was reactivated on August 5, mostly appearing as designated hitter in the following month, before making more regular appearances in the outfield from mid-September.[243] On September 9 against the Detroit Tigers, Judge hit his 359th home run, passing Yogi Berra for fifth place on the Yankees all-time home run list.[244] On September 11, two more home runs against the Tigers brought his total to 361, tying Joe DiMaggio for fourth all-time in Yankees history.[245] The next day, he passed DiMaggio with his 362nd against the Red Sox.[246] This was his 19th first-inning home run of the season, breaking a tie with Alex Rodriguez (2001) and his own mark from 2024 for the most home runs by a player in a single inning in a season.[247] On September 15, Judge was named the AL Player of the Week after going 9-for-20 with a 1.760 OPS, five home runs, and five RBIs.[248] On September 24, he hit two home runs against the Chicago White Sox, bringing him to the 50 home run mark in a season for the fourth time, tying Babe Ruth, Mark McGwire, and Sammy Sosa for the most such seasons in MLB history. Judge and Ruth are the only Yankees to have consecutive years of 50 or more homers.[249] On September 29, Judge was named the AL Player of the Week after going 9-for-20 with four home runs, nine RBIs and an OPS of 1.693.[250] He also won the AL Player of the Month for September after slashing .391/.440/.772 with 10 home runs.[251] Judge finished the regular season with a .331 batting average, leading the major leagues, and winning his first career batting title. At 6 feet 7 inches, he became the tallest batting champion in MLB history.[252] He slashed .331/.457/.688 with 53 home runs, 179 hits, two triples, 30 doubles, 114 RBI, 124 walks, 36 intentional walks, and 12 stolen bases in 152 games.[253] His 53 home runs were the most ever by a batting champion.[254] Judge's 36 intentional walks broke the AL single-season record, overtaking Ted Williams' record of 34 set in the 1957 season.[255] In the AL Wild Card Series against the Boston Red Sox, Judge went 4-for-11 with one walk and one RBI.[256] On October 7 at Yankee Stadium, with the Yankees facing being swept in the AL Division Series by the Toronto Blue Jays, Judge went 3-4 and hit the tying three-run home run off the foul pole as the Yankees came back from a 6–1 run deficit to keep the series alive.[257] This effort answered some of the lingering criticism of his previously underwhelming postseason performances, which had amplified after he had struck out with the bases loaded in the series opener.[258] Judge ended the postseason slashing .500/.581/.692 with 13 hits, one home run, two doubles and seven RBI across seven games as the Yankees were eliminated by the Blue Jays in four games.[259] Judge won his third career and second consecutive AL MVP award. Judge chose the nickname "All Rise" (given to him by former teammate Todd Frazier) to be worn on the back of his jersey nameplate.[295] For the 2018 Players Weekend, Judge chose to just have his last name on the back of his jersey.[296] During the 2019 event, Judge chose his own nickname, "BAJ", an abbreviation of "Big Aaron Judge".[297] Player profile Judge is listed at 6 feet 7 inches (2.01 m) and 282 pounds (128 kg).[298] Due to his large size and strength, he has elicited comparisons to teammate Giancarlo Stanton, as well as former MLB players Richie Sexson,[299] Dave Winfield, and Willie Stargell.[28] His power output is historically significant: he is one of only eight players in MLB history to record an OPS+ above 200 in three seasons.[300][165] He is also one of just 14 players in MLB history to slug above .700 in a season with a minimum of 550 plate appearances.[301] He is the fourth player ever to produce four seasons with 50 or more home runs, alongside Ruth, Sammy Sosa, and Mark McGwire.[302] Since Statcast began tracking exit velocity in 2015, Judge broke the record for the hardest-hit home run with a 121.1 mph blast on June 10, 2017, against the Baltimore Orioles; although later surpassed, it ranked fifth as of 2025.[303] Judge and Miguel Sanó are the only MLB players since 2015 to hit multiple home runs measuring at least 495 feet.[304] Pregame rituals Judge has been credited as a team leader both on and off the field.[305] He has a number of rituals before, during, and after games. According to Michael Kay (as discussed during The Michael Kay Show), before each game at Yankee Stadium, Judge ceremoniously tosses exactly 40 sunflower seeds in the grass behind home plate (one for each man on the extended Yankees roster.)[306] The prayer he recites after he tosses the seeds is unknown to the public.[306] During his 2017 rookie season, it was reported that Judge kept a note on his phone that read ".179", his batting average with the Yankees in 2016, and looked at it daily as a source of motivation.[307] Appearances outside of baseball Philanthropy Judge is the founder of the ALL RISE Foundation.
