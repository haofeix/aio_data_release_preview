WORLD BASEBALL CLASSIC MOST VALUABLE PLAYER AWARD The World Baseball Classic (WBC) emerged in 2006 as Major League Baseball's most ambitious attempt to create a truly global championship—one that could stand alongside the World Cup in soccer and the World Championships long overseen by the International Baseball Federation. Prior to the WBC, international baseball's major stages were fragmented: Olympic baseball (1992, 1996, 2000, 2004, 2008, and 2020) and the long-running Baseball World Cup (discontinued after 2011) drew passionate followings but rarely featured the sport's top professional talent. The WBC changed that dynamic immediately. Backed by MLB, the MLBPA, and federations around the world, it became the first tournament in which nations could field full-strength rosters of major leaguers, rising prospects, and elite professionals from Asia, Latin America, and beyond. From its inaugural edition, the Classic established itself as a showcase of national pride and high-pressure baseball. Japan's back-to-back championships in 2006 and 2009 set the early standard, while the Dominican Republic's undefeated run in 2013 and the United States' breakthrough title in 2017 demonstrated how quickly the competitive landscape could shift. By 2023, the WBC had grown into one of the most-watched baseball events on the planet, drawing massive global audiences and producing moments—such as Shohei Ohtani striking out Mike Trout for the final out—that instantly entered the sport's international canon. Within this expanding history, the World Baseball Classic Most Valuable Player Award has become one of the tournament's defining honors. Unlike MLB's season-long MVP, the WBC version recognizes a player whose performance elevates an entire nation's hopes over a concentrated, pressure-filled slate of games. Daisuke Matsuzaka set the tone by winning the first two MVP awards, anchoring Japan's early dominance with unmatched consistency on the mound. Robinson Canó became the first position player to claim the honor in 2013, powering the Dominican Republic's perfect run. Marcus Stroman's brilliance in 2017 helped deliver the United States its first title, and Shohei Ohtani's two-way heroics in 2023 produced one of the most complete tournament performances ever recorded. As the Classic continues to evolve, the WBC MVP Award stands as a record of the players who not only excelled individually, but who shaped the global narrative of baseball’s most international stage.
