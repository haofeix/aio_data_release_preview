Plot[] In 1954, struggling Prince Castle salesman Ray Kroc travels to San Bernardino, California meet Dick and Mac McDonald at their eponymous drive-in restaurant after the brothers purchase eight of his multimixer milkshake machines. Kroc lauds the brothers' success over dinner. Mac and Dick explain the restaurant's origins and success: The McDonald brothers moved from New Hampshire and brought up an indoor theater in Hollywood wanting to start a movie business, but they were forced to withdraw because of the immediate 1929 stock market crash. A hot dog vendor then inspired the brothers to open their own hamburger stand. After a complete redesign of the restaurant in 1948, eliminating unnecessary overhead, preventing hangouts for juvenile delinquents, and cutting costs. Establishing speed food-service systems has revolutionized the food industry, high-quality food, disposable packaging and a family-friendly environment. However, many customers expected carhops to serve them like other drive-in restaurants. The brothers persisted, keeping their menu simple; miraculously appealed to drivers on the go who could get a quick hamburger meal with no more waiting. Eager to cash in, Kroc presses the brothers to expand franchising. After an initial refusal, the brothers agree to a business deal with stringent contract terms and a lengthy approval process for potential changes. Kroc breaks ground in Des Plaines, Illinois putting up his home as collateral. Hungry for more growth, Kroc pursues wealthy local connections for investment in franchising and recruits Fred L. After early struggles with franchise operators not being involved, which had doomed the brothers' previous franchise attempts, Ray hit the model of the local operator: putting the franchisee directly into the workspace and working alongside their staff. Kroc sees rapid gains and continues to expand, traveling to St. Paul oversee the first opening in the Twin Cities. There, Kroc meets Rollie Smith, and his wife, Joan, who are interested in franchising; Kroc immediately becomes infatuated with Joan. At the same time, Kroc is stressed out over rising pressure from financial operating costs and falls behind on his payments. Kroc is unsuccessful at renegotiating his contract with the McDonald brothers, and, when his bank forecloses on his home, Kroc's wife, Ethel, discovers her husband put the house up as collateral without her knowledge.
