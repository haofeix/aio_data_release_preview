From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia Killing of Rob and Michele ReinerMichele and Rob Reiner in 2016LocationBrentwood, Los Angeles,California, U.S.DateDecember 14, 2025; 3 months agoAttack typeHomicideVictimsRob ReinerMichele Singer ReinerAccusedNick ReinerChargesFirst-degree murder withspecial circumstances (×2) On December 14, 2025, American filmmaker Rob Reiner and his wife, photographer and producer Michele Singer Reiner, were found dead with multiple sharp force injuries at their home in Brentwood, a neighborhood in Los Angeles, California. Their son, Nick Reiner, was arrested later that day on suspicion of murder. The case against him is ongoing. Rob Reiner was an American actor, director, and political activist. His credits include This Is Spinal Tap, The Princess Bride, When Harry Met Sally..., Stand by Me, and A Few Good Men.[1] Michele Singer Reiner was a photographer, producer, and LGBTQ rights advocate.[2] Rob and Michele married in 1989 and had three children.[3][1] Rob also had an older adopted daughter, Tracy, whose mother was his former wife, Penny Marshall.[1] Rob was 78 years old and Michele was 70 years old[a] at the time of their deaths.[3] Nick Reiner in 2016 Nicholas Reiner (born September 14, 1993)[4] is the second of three children: he has an older brother, Jake, and a younger sister, Romy.[5] He was born and raised in Los Angeles.[6] He attended Wildwood, a private school in West Los Angeles.[7] Reiner has a GED credential but decided not to attend college.[6] Before his arrest, Nick Reiner had discussed long-running substance abuse problems in interviews and podcasts, including periods in and out of treatment beginning in his adolescence.[8] He shared a room at one of the treatment centers with a boy who was addicted to heroin, a drug that Nick would try in later years.[9] The Irish Times, summarizing prior public accounts, reported that Nick estimated he entered drug treatment eighteen times as a teenager, and spoke about addiction to heroin and cocaine.[10] Rob Reiner said that reading David Sheff's memoir Beautiful Boy: A Father's Journey Through His Son's Addiction educated him about his son's situation.[11] Nick had an often contentious relationship with his parents, who committed many resources toward his recovery.[9] In 2015, Nick Reiner co-wrote the film Being Charlie, which was inspired by his experiences with addiction and his relationship with his father, Rob Reiner, who directed the film.[12] On December 16, 2025, People magazine reported that Nick had a long history of personal struggles, which included erratic behavior, and becoming a drug user at a young age. Nick's first stint at a drug rehab was when he was 14 years old. He was in and out of rehab starting at age 15, culminating a total of eighteen stays in rehab by 2016. Nick also became homeless.[13] Despite the constant cycle of active addiction and stays in rehab centers, Nick reportedly became closer to his father during the production of Being Charlie, a film he helped write.[13] Nevertheless, police visited the Reiner home on many occasions over the following decade due to incidents involving Nick.[14][15] Nick was placed into a yearlong mental health conservatorship in 2020. The conservatorship ended in 2021.[16] At the time of their deaths, Nick was living with his parents.[13] The night before Rob and Michele Reiner were discovered dead, the couple and Nick had attended a Christmas party hosted by Conan O'Brien, but left after Nick, who reportedly "was freaking everyone out, acting crazy, [and] kept asking people if they were famous" and got into a "very loud argument" with Rob.[17] During the argument, Rob reportedly chastised his son for his inappropriate conduct.[18] Another associate of the Reiner family disputed the intensity of the argument and told The New York Times that the Reiners would not have left the party early due to Nick's behavior, which the family had grown used to.[18] In one incident at the party, Nick Reiner interrupted Bill Hader who was having a conversation with two other guests. After Hader told him that the conversation was private, Reiner allegedly stood still and stared before "storming off".[19] Killings and investigation[edit] On the afternoon of December 14, Rob and Michele Reiner's daughter Romy discovered her father's body in the master bedroom of their Brentwood home.[13][18][20] Her roommate, with whom she lived across the street from her parents' house, called 911 at 3:38 p.m. Pacific Standard Time after Romy fled the residence.[18][21] Shortly after, Romy contacted Billy Crystal and his wife Janice, close friends of the Reiner family, who arrived at the scene as emergency services were attending to the victims.[22] Los Angeles Fire Department paramedics responding to the call found a man and a woman dead inside the house.[1] Romy was reportedly unaware of her mother's death before paramedics arrived.[18] Los Angeles Police Department officers responded shortly afterward for what the department described as a death investigation that later developed into a homicide case.[3][23] The investigation was handled by the LAPD's robbery and homicide division, and police stated detectives were working to obtain a search warrant as part of the investigation.[1] On December 17, the Los Angeles County Department of Medical Examiner released the cause of death for the Reiners; both Rob and Michele's cause of death was multiple sharp force injuries inflicted with a knife,[24][25] and the manner of death was homicide.[26][27][28][29] The death certificates also revealed they died "within minutes" of the attack.[30][28][31] Arrest and legal proceedings[edit] Nick Reiner was arrested on the night of December 14 near the University of Southern California.[32][3][23] Police initially listed bail at $4 million, before announcing he would instead be held without bail.[3] The case had not yet been presented to prosecutors.[23] Later that day, he was charged by Los Angeles District Attorney Nathan Hochman with two counts of first-degree murder with a special circumstance of multiple murders.[5][33] Hochman said that they have not yet decided whether to pursue the death penalty.[34] On December 17, Nick made his first court appearance with his defense attorney Alan Jackson, known for his work as both the prosecuting and defense attorney on cases involving celebrities and other high-profile matters.[35] At his first court appearance, Nick only spoke briefly to a judge, did not enter a plea, and waived his right to a speedy trial[36][37] after Jackson asked for a continuance regarding Nick's arraignment.[36][38] He is being held at the Twin Towers Correctional Facility,[39] and his arraignment was set for January 7, 2026.[30][40] On January 7, during the scheduled arraignment, Jackson withdrew from the case. Nick was then appointed a public defender and his arraignment was subsequently postponed to February 23.[41][42] Nick pleaded not guilty in court on February 23.[43][44] He was denied bail and ordered to remain in custody.[45][46] He was ordered to appear in court again on April 29 for a preliminary hearing.[47][48] Tributes and reaction[edit] Rob and Michele's other two children, Jake and Romy, released a joint statement on December 17, 2025, calling their parents their "best friends".[49] Close friends and collaborators Albert Brooks, Billy Crystal, Larry David, Martin Short, Barry Levinson, Marc Shaiman, and Alan Zweibel released a joint statement which read in part, "There is no other director who has his range...he was always at the top of his game. He charmed audiences. They trusted him." They also wrote, "Michele and Rob Reiner devoted a great deal of their lives for the betterment of our fellow citizens ... They were a special force together—dynamic, unselfish and inspiring. We were their friends, and we will miss them forever".[50][51] Director Martin Scorsese's personal essay remembering Rob and Michele Reiner was published in The New York Times on Christmas Day; he wrote about how he met Reiner through mutual friends in 1970s Los Angeles, regretting the need to use the past tense in describing them, and saying "I loved hanging out with Rob. We had a natural affinity for each other. He was hilarious and sometimes bitingly funny, but he was never the kind of guy who would take over the room."[52] In January 2026, Mel Brooks, who had been best friends with Rob Reiner's father Carl Reiner, commented at a premiere screening of the documentary Mel Brooks: The 99 Year Old Man!, "Carl, with what's happened, I'm glad that he passed away when he did...I mean, he never could have survived this terrible, terrible thing."[53] Numerous figures in the entertainment industry also paid tribute to Reiner, including Sean Astin, Kevin Bacon, Alec Baldwin, Kathy Bates, Jamie Lee Curtis, John Cusack, Michael Douglas, Zooey Deschanel, Cary Elwes, Jane Fonda, Morgan Freeman, Whoopi Goldberg, Ron Howard, Eric Idle, Stephen King, Jerry Seinfeld, Julia Louis-Dreyfus, Demi Moore, Meg Ryan, Harry Shearer, Ben Stiller, Barbra Streisand, Arnold Schwarzenegger, James Woods, and Robin Wright.[13][54][55][56] Sally Struthers, who played his on-screen wife in All In the Family, called the deaths "beyond devastating."[57] Cast members from Stand by Me, including Corey Feldman, Jerry O'Connell, and Wil Wheaton, also issued statements remembering Reiner.[58][59][60] Late-night hosts including Jimmy Fallon, Jimmy Kimmel, Seth Meyers, and Howard Stern also paid tribute.[61][62] Los Angeles mayor Karen Bass called Reiner's death a "devastating loss for our city and our country", citing his creative work and advocacy for social justice.[63] California governor Gavin Newsom said he was "heartbroken", praising Reiner's "boundless empathy" and his advocacy for children and civil rights.[64] Three ex-presidents issued condolences: former U.S. president Bill Clinton released a statement describing the Reiners as friends and praising their public service.[65][54] Former president Barack Obama said that he and his wife Michelle were "heartbroken", noting Reiner's "deep belief in the goodness of people"; Michelle noted they had planned to meet the Reiners in person on the day of their deaths.[55][66] Former president Joe Biden and former vice president Kamala Harris also issued statements mourning the couple and praising their character.[67][68] Political commentator and strategist James Carville described Reiner as "a friend of mine" and also someone who held a huge "cultural impact" on his life.[69][70] Journalists paying tribute to Reiner included CNN's Anderson Cooper.[71] President Donald Trump mocked Reiner, writing on Truth Social that "a very sad thing happened" and stating Reiner and his wife died "reportedly due to the anger he caused others through his massive, unyielding, and incurable affliction with a mind crippling disease known as Trump derangement syndrome".[72][73][74][75] Trump's reaction was widely criticized[76][77] by both Republicans and Democrats;
