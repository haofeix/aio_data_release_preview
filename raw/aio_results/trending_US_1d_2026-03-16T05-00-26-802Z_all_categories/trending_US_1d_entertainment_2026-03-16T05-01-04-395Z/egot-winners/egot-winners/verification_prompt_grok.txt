See a full list of the entertainment luminaries who became EGOT winners by snagging an Emmy, a Grammy, an Oscar, and a Tony—sometimes more than one of each.Clockwise: Mel Brooks at the 1998 Emmy Awards; John Legend at the 2006 Grammy Awards; Whoopi Goldberg at the 1991 Oscars; Andrew Lloyd Webber at the 2018 Tony Awards; Barbra Streisand at the 1987 Grammy Awards; Audrey Hepburn at the 1968 Tony Awards.All from Getty Images.What is an EGOT? Technically, it’s the term for someone who has won an Emmy, a Grammy, an Oscar, and a Tony. But more significantly, the acronym signifies an entertainer who has dominated all forms of entertainment, from stage and screen to music and television. It’s a major achievement to win even one of these prizes, but to win all of the top prizes in entertainment is to join an upper echelon of legends. Why settle for “Look Mom, I won a [insert one top prize of your choice here]!” when you can overachieve your way to a full trophy closet, and a place in the history books?How many EGOT winners are there? There are 21 EGOT winners—27 if you include EGOTs with noncompetitive awards, such as lifetime achievement honors—including two winners who have EGOT-ed twice. Here is the list of every EGOT winner, in the order in which they EGOT-ed.Richard Rogers – May 22, 1962The first ever EGOT winner was none other than half of the composing team that gave us such timeless musicals as The Sound of Music and South Pacific, which respectively earned him his first Grammy (best show album—original cast) and Tony (best musical and best score). His and Oscar Hammerstein II’s State Fair song “It Might as Well Be Spring” earned the best-song Academy Award. EGOT, an acronym for the Emmy, Grammy, Oscar, and Tony Awards, is the designation given to people who have won all four of the major performing art awards in the United States.[1][2] Respectively, these awards honor outstanding achievements in television, audio recording/music, film, and Broadway theatre.[3] Achieving the EGOT has been referred to as the "grand slam" of American show business.[1][4] Including those with honorary or special awards, 28 people have achieved this status.[5] Only one person, Robert Lopez, has won all four awards twice.[6] The EGOT acronym was coined by actor Philip Michael Thomas in late 1984. While starring in Miami Vice, he stated a desire to achieve the EGOT within five years.[7][8] The acronym gained wider recognition following a 2009 episode of 30 Rock that introduced EGOT status as a recurring plotline.[9] There is some debate over whether only the Primetime Emmy Award should count towards an EGOT, as some (including Thomas himself) distinguish the other types of Emmy competitions as subordinate to the Primetime honor.[10] Starting in 2016, the Daytime Emmy Awards had a category for Outstanding Musical Performance in a Daytime Program, which was removed after the 2019 ceremony because three of the four winners were Broadway ensembles, which between them included five people (Cynthia Erivo, Rachel Bay Jones, Katrina Lenk, Ben Platt, and Ari'el Stachel) who had already won Tony and Grammy awards for the shows they were in, and with their Daytime Emmy wins only needed Oscars to complete their EGOT status.[11] In 2023, TheaterMania writer Zachary Stewart criticized the practice of "selling" producer credits for shows favored to win a Tony as a "shortcut" to EGOT status. Another variation is the REGOT, which includes being awarded a Razzie Award.[50][51] Alan Menken has a REGOT due to his Razzie win (with Jack Feldman) for Worst Original Song for "High Times, Hard Times" from Newsies.[52] With her Razzie win for Worst Actress for Rent-a-Cop and Arthur 2: On the Rocks, Liza Minnelli has a REGOT if her non-competitive Grammy Legend Award is considered.[53] Lady Gaga reordered the acronym as EGORT, when she hosted Saturday Night Live and poked fun at her win for Worst Screen Combo (with Joaquin Phoenix) from Joker: Folie à Deux.[54] Alternatively, publications such as Vulture have listed the REGOT as including an induction into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame.[55] The only person to have such a REGOT is Elton John although Harry Belafonte and Quincy Jones would be included if counting non-competitive awards. Equivalent honors outside the United States[edit] The Emmy, Grammy, Oscar, and Tony Awards are presided over by industry bodies based in the United States, and as of 2026, 17 out of the 22 EGOT winners were American nationals. The remaining five―John Gielgud, Audrey Hepburn, Elton John, Andrew Lloyd Webber, and Tim Rice―were British. Many countries hold their own equivalent awards ceremonies honouring their own television, music, film, and theatre industries. In some cases, commentators in other countries have derived their own acronyms for individuals who have won at all four ceremonies. In 2018, Leah Collins of CBC Arts proposed a Canadian equivalent of the EGOT: the Canadian Screen Awards (and their predecessors, the Gemini Awards and the Genie Awards) for film and television, the Juno Awards for music, and the Dora Mavor Moore Awards for theatre.[56] Toronto-based game show Trivia Club referred to this combination as the "Two-Can-Ju-Do".[57] No individual has won in all four categories. In 2019, Caitlin Welsh of Nova Entertainment proposed the "LAHA" as an Australian equivalent: the Logie Awards for television, the ARIA Music Awards for music, the Helpmann Awards for theatre, and the AACTA Awards for film.[58] She also could not identify any winners of all four awards, although Noni Hazlehurst has received nominations in all four.
