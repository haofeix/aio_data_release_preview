Jessie Buckley (born December 28, 1989, Killarney, Ireland) is an Irish actress and singer, best known for her performance as Agnes (Anne) Hathaway, the wife of William Shakespeare, in the film Hamnet (2025), for which she won the Academy Award for best actress. Buckley began her career on the BBC talent show I’d Do Anything in 2008 and has since built a formidable reputation on stage and screen, including for her roles in the acclaimed miniseries Chernobyl (2019), a 2016 BBC TV adaptation of War and Peace, and the films Wild Rose (2018) and The Lost Daughter (2021). Early life Buckley, the eldest of five children, was born in Killarney, Ireland, to Tim Buckley, a hotel bar manager who published poetry, and Marina Cassidy, a vocal coach and music psychotherapist who also sang professionally and played the harp. The Buckley family had no television, and Jessie Buckley grew up acting in school musicals, including playing the male lead in West Side Story, and learning music—including the harp, piano, saxophone, and clarinet—at the Royal Irish Academy of Music (RIAM). In 2001 she received a RIAM High Achiever Award. At age 17 Buckley applied unsuccessfully to drama schools before choosing to audition for the BBC talent show I’d Do Anything in 2008, with the goal of being cast as Nancy in the West End revival of Oliver!, a musical adaptation of the classic Charles Dickens novel Oliver Twist. Although Buckley finished as the runner-up to actress Jodie Prenger and later turned down an offer to be Prenger’s understudy, her performance impressed the judges Cameron Mackintosh and Andrew Lloyd Webber, who urged her to attend the Royal Academy of Dramatic Art (RADA) and participate in a four-week-long William Shakespeare course. Buckley received a bachelor’s degree from RADA in 2013. Acting career and transition from theater to screen Buckley played the role of Anne Egerman in the 2008 revival of Stephen Sondheim’s musical A Little Night Music, and she continued with stage roles after graduating from RADA. In 2013 she played Miranda in The Tempest and Princess Katharine in Henry V, opposite Jude Law, both works by Shakespeare. In 2015 Buckley appeared alongside Judi Dench in The Winter’s Tale. She first appeared on the small screen as an episodic character in the TV shows This September (2010–11) and Endeavour (2014), followed by an appearance in a podcast episode of Doctor Who (2015). In 2016 her performance as Marya Bolkonskaya in a TV miniseries adaptation of Leo Tolstoy’s War and Peace was well received. She shared the screen with actor Tom Hardy in the 2017 TV series Taboo, before her film debut in Beast the same year. Buckley then appeared in the miniseries The Last Post (2017) and The Woman in White (2018), the latter based on a novel by Wilkie Collins. Her portrayal of an aspiring country music star who has just been released from a Glasgow, Scotland, prison in the 2018 film Wild Rose is considered her breakthrough performance; the film allowed Buckley to showcase her acting and singing talent, and she was nominated for a BAFTA Award. She gained further exposure after appearing in the acclaimed miniseries Chernobyl (2019), a dramatization of the 1986 nuclear disaster in the Soviet Union. She also played Queen Victoria in the 2020 film Dolittle, starring Robert Downey, Jr. “From the moment I read her, there was something birdlike about her, a rhythm. She listened to recordings of people from Minnesota to prepare for the character’s American Upper Midwest accent and worked with a movement coach to perfect the character’s style of walking. Also in 2020 she appeared opposite Jesse Plemons in the Charlie Kaufman psychological horror film I’m Thinking of Ending Things. From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia Jessie BuckleyBuckley in 2025BornDecember 1989 (age 36)Killarney, County Kerry, IrelandAlma materRoyal Academy of Dramatic Art (BA)OccupationsActresssingerYears active2008–presentSpouse Freddie Sorensen ​(m. 2023)​Children1RelativesMadge Clifford (great-grandmother)AwardsFull list Jessie Buckley (born December 1989[1]) is an Irish actress and singer. Her accolades include an Academy Award, two BAFTA Awards, an Actor Award, a Golden Globe Award, a Critics' Choice Award and a Laurence Olivier Award. Buckley began her acting career in 2008 as a contestant on the BBC talent show I'd Do Anything,[2] in which she came second. A Royal Academy of Dramatic Art graduate, she made her early onscreen appearances in BBC television series such as War & Peace (2016) and Taboo (2017). Buckley made her film debut with the lead role in Beast (2017), followed by her breakthrough role as an aspiring country music singer in the musical film Wild Rose (2018); the latter earned her a nomination for the BAFTA Award for Best Actress in a Leading Role. Buckley's career progressed with starring roles in films such as I'm Thinking of Ending Things (2020), Men (2022), Women Talking (2022) and Wicked Little Letters (2023). For her performance as a troubled mother in the psychological drama The Lost Daughter (2021), she received nominations for the BAFTA Award for Best Actress in a Supporting Role and for the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress. She gained further recognition for her portrayal of Agnes Shakespeare in the period drama Hamnet (2025), receiving the Golden Globe, BAFTA Award, Actor Award and Academy Award for Best Actress. On television, Buckley has starred in the HBO miniseries Chernobyl (2019) and season four of Fargo (2020). On stage, Buckley's portrayal of Sally Bowles in a 2021 West End theatre revival of the musical Cabaret won her the Laurence Olivier Award for Best Actress in a Musical. In 2022, she released the collaborative album For All Our Days That Tear the Heart with Bernard Butler, which was shortlisted for the 2022 Mercury Prize. Early life and education[edit] Jessie Buckley was born in December 1989[1] in Killarney, County Kerry,[1] to Marina (née Cassidy) and Tim Buckley.[3] She has a younger brother and three younger sisters.[1] She is the great-granddaughter of Madge Clifford, an Irish republican.[4] She attended Ursuline Secondary School, an all-girls convent school in Thurles, County Tipperary, where her mother works as a vocal coach[5][1] and where she performed in school productions.[1] She played a number of male roles at school, including the male lead role of Tony in the musical West Side Story and Freddie Trumper in Chess.[6] She reached grade eight in piano, clarinet and harp at the Royal Irish Academy of Music and was a member of the Tipperary Millennium Orchestra. She attended summer workshops with the Association of Irish Musical Societies (AIMS) to improve her singing and acting. It was there that she was recognised as a talented actress and encouraged to apply for drama school in London. In an interview with British Vogue, Buckley said: "I really hope that a 15, 17, whatever-age woman never has to be brutalised quite like what happened on that show. But I didn't recognise it fully at the time. I just felt it, which was difficult."[10] On 26 August 2008, Buckley performed on Denny Street in Tralee, County Kerry, for RTÉ Radio 1.[citation needed] She followed this with a performance at a charity concert in County Tipperary.[citation needed] On 14 September 2008, Buckley performed at an outdoor concert in celebration of Andrew Lloyd Webber's birthday in Hyde Park, London. She gave a solo rendition of "I Don't Know How To Love Him" from Jesus Christ Superstar and sang "Light at the End of the Tunnel" from Starlight Express with fellow I'd Do Anything finalists Keisha Amponsa-Banson, Niamh Perry and Rachel Tucker, as well as Any Dream Will Do finalists Daniel Boys, Lewis Bradley, Ben James-Ellis and Keith Jack.[11] On 18 September 2008, she and Aoife Mulholland performed with the RTÉ Concert Orchestra at another event held by Lloyd Webber at the National Concert Hall, Dublin.[12] Buckley was offered the opportunity to understudy for the role of Nancy,[13] but turned it down in favour of another production. Jessie Buckley began her career in 2008 as a contestant on the BBC TV talent show I'd Do Anything, in which she placed second. In the same year, she appeared in the West End revival of Stephen Sondheim's A Little Night Music. She then studied at the Royal Academy of Dramatic Art, from which she graduated in 2013. Her early onscreen appearances include portrayals in BBC television series, such as Marya Bolkonskaya in the 2016 adaptation of Leo Tolstoy's War and Peace, Lorna Bow in Taboo (2017) and Marian Halcombe in The Woman in White (2018). Buckley made her film debut playing the lead role of Moll Huntford in Beast (2017). Her other roles include Rose-Lynn Harlan in Wild Rose (2018), for which she received a nomination for the BAFTA Award for Best Actress in a Leading Role, Lyudmilla Ignatenko in the HBO miniseries Chernobyl (2019), the Young Woman in I'm Thinking of Ending Things (2020), Oraetta Mayflower in season four of Fargo (2020) and young Leda in The Lost Daughter (2021). Her performance in The Lost Daughter gained her a nomination for the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress. In 2019, she was recognised by Forbes in its annual 30 Under 30 list. In 2020, she was listed at No. 38 on The Irish Times' list of Ireland's greatest film actors. Buckley starred as Sally Bowles in a West End revival of Cabaret in 2021, for which she received an Olivier Award nomination for Best Actress in a Musical. She received further acclaim for her supporting role in Sarah Polley's Oscar-winning drama Women Talking and will next star in the title role of Maggie Gyllenhaal's The Bride, co-starring Christian Bale and Penelope Cruz. In 2023 Jessie starred alongside Jeremy Allen White in the Apple TV drama series Fingernails which led to an IFTA nomination for her performance, following this Jessie starred alongside Olivia Coleman once again for Wicked Little Letters, her performance gained another IFTA Nomination in 2025.
