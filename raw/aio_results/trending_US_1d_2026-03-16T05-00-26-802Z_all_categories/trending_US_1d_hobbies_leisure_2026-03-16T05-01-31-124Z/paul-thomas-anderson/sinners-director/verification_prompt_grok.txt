The film entered the night with 13 nominations — the most ever for a project directed by a Black filmmaker in BAFTA history — and converted three of them into wins: Best Supporting Actress for Wunmi Mosaku, Best Original Score for Ludwig Göransson, and Best Original Screenplay for Coogler. [R3] "Sinners" Director Ryan Coogler: A Career Retrospective
(https://www.buzzfeed.com/pernellquilon/ryan-coogler-appreciation)
"""
Director Ryan Coogler, the man behind Sinners (2025) and Black Panther (2018) made history when he won the BAFTA for Best Original Screenplay for his film Sinners, becoming the first Black person to be awarded in the category. BBC / Via youtube.com The win feels extra special knowing that 21 years ago, Coogler wasn't even thinking about films — he was on a football scholarship at a California college when his creative writing professor was so impressed with his first assignment that she suggested he look into writing screenplays. At first, he shrugged it off. Then, he thought, "Why not?"Now, he has a packed roster of projects ahead of him that are bound to break further ground in Hollywood. Today, Coogler is undeniably one of the hottest names in film; if he's attached to a project, it's getting made. Pretty wild since his first feature came out just 13 years ago. Before that, he was getting started at the USC School of Cinematic Arts in 2008. Leon Bennett / Getty Images / East Bay Times Right before he started USC's prestigious graduate film program, his hometown newspaper dubbed him the "Spike Lee of the East Bay," a prophecy in hindsight — Lee has helped mentor Coogler in his Hollywood journey, and the two directors are close. So, in honor of Coogler's real-time legacy, here's a look back at all the films he's directed, starting from his first short film: * Well, all the films we could track down. There are mentions of two short films, Story of a Dollar and Eyes Like Mine, that he created even before USC — there is no trace of them online. I looked. 1. Locks (2009) follows a young man with locks walking through Oakland to the barbershop; on his way, he encounters other Black men being handcuffed by the police, followed by his friends hanging out on the block. The short was screened at the prestigious Tribeca Film Festival, and contains elements explored throughout Coogler's filmography: the over-policing of Black neighborhoods, the joy and intimacy shared within them, and his hometown of Oakland itself. Locks was the first of a collection of shorts Coogler directed while he was getting his MFA at USC's School of Cinematic Arts. University of Southern California / Via vimeo.com You can watch it here. 2. The Gap (2010) and The Sculptor (2011) are two other shorts Coogler filmed while he was in USC's graduate film program, and while no trace of either can be found online, the source material behind The Gap, a scene from a play, is. In the scene, a public school teacher questions if her Black student actually wrote his impressive essay since the way he writes doesn't match the way he speaks — it's ripe for social commentary, something Coogler excitingly takes on in his next short and for the rest of his career. Chung Sung-jun / Getty Images / Bloomsbury Publishing / Google Books 3. Fig (2010) is the final of the short films he created while at USC, and it follows the journey of a loving single mother who returns to the streets as a sex worker to help provide for her kid. And when her daughter's taken by child protective services, she doesn't hesitate to turn a new leaf and show up for her girl...but is still met with a tragic end. So much stands out about this short, though Coogler's ability to humanize people, who are often vilified in real life, through his use of close-ups and how he lets the camera linger on them to catch them in private moments, is really outstanding. And he brings this energy to his following project: his debut feature. Ryan Coogler / Via Kanopy Fig screened at the 2010 Tribeca Film Festival and won the HBO Short Filmmaking Award, DGA Student Filmmaker Award, and was also nominated for the Black Reel Award for Outstanding Independent Short Film. You can watch the award-winning short here on Kanopy (free with your local library card). 4. Following Fig, Coogler applied to the Sundance Institute with a powerful script of a "story he needed to tell" — two years later, his first feature film, Fruitvale Station (2013), premiered at the famous Sundance Film Festival. The film, based on a true story, follows the last 24 hours of Oscar Grant III, an unarmed Black man who was shot and killed by a police officer in Oakland on New Year's Day in 2009. It won the Grand Jury Prize and Audience Award at Sundance, and went on to win Best First Film at the Cannes Film Festival. It's a powerful film full of love alongside its tragic ending, and it shone an earned spotlight on first-time director Coogler and his star, then-unknown actor Michael B. Jordan — this was the beginning of their long-term collaborative partnership. OG Project , The Weinstein Company So much of this movie stuck with me, though one scene that really stayed with me is the 36-second continuous shot of Oscar, played by Jordan, walking through the empty daycare center to pick up his daughter. This quiet private moment, that in writing seems so mundane, is so intimate that it really humanizes Oscar — it almost feels like a reminder that even though he was grown, he was still a 22-year-old with an entire life ahead of him. Fruitvale Station is packed with powerful performances (including Octavia Spencer) that are all worth watching. It's available online, and you can rent or purchase it through Apple TV, Amazon, and YouTube. 5. Before Creed ever hit theaters, Coogler laid the groundwork with From Rocky to Creed: The Legacy Continues (2015), a documentary that retells the Cinderella-like story of Rocky, a movie with humble beginnings and an incredibly low budget, and then bridges that legacy into Creed. The doc, narrated by Michael B. Jordan, features interviews with creator Sylvester Stallone, original cast members, and producers. It traces how the underdog tale became an American myth, followed by an inside look at how Coogler approached Creed’s continuation of that story. At its surface, it's an hour-long promo for Creed. Underneath that, it's a showcase of Coogler’s meticulous sensibilities and how he honors source material while infusing it with fresh emotional depth, a balance he first displayed in Fruitvale Station and would fully realize in Creed. Associated Television International , EPIX From Rocky to Creed originally aired on EPIX in 2015 and was available to stream on Hulu until 2017 — it can currently be watched online here. 6. The story behind Creed (2015) is just as inspiring as the movie itself. The Rocky franchise sparked Coogler's interest in filmmaking and was his father's favorite films. Coogler came up with the idea for the spin-off when his dad was diagnosed with a neuromuscular condition — if you haven't seen the film, Creed follows the son of Apollo Creed making a name for himself in the boxing world under the guidance of the legendary Rocky, who's fighting his own battle: a cancer diagnosis. Coogler co-wrote the script with USC classmate and friend Aaron Covington; the stars aligned for Coogler to meet with Sylvester Stallone and pitch him the idea, and the rest is history. The film was a smash hit, leading Marvel to tap the fairly new director to take on their next superhero film. MGM Creed was a hit with critics and audiences, grossing over $173.6 million against a $35 million budget. The film feels just as much of a love letter to Philadelphia as it does to the original Rocky movies, and Coogler's creative voice in it was just as pervasive as it is in his short films and Fruitvale Station — he doesn't shy away from addressing the often limited options Black people have to escape systemic generational poverty ("We're not singing! We're not dancing! We're fighting!") and portrays neighborhoods stereotypically seen as "rough" to be brimming with love, culture, and kinship.Coogler excels at lingering in the private, quiet moments of a hero's journey (the long, continuous take prevails — a one-shot of Creed walking from backstage to the boxing ring clocks in at over two minutes) and at building out the cities his characters live in. Fortunately for us, Coogler takes these auteur abilities with him to his next milestone: the Marvel Cinematic Universe.Creed is available to rent on YouTube, Apple TV, and Prime Video. 7. If for any reason you don't know Coogler, you definitely know Black Panther (2018) — the film was a phenomenon globally, bringing in over $1.35 billion, becoming the highest-grossing solo superhero film of all time and the highest-grossing film by a Black director (not to mention the highest-grossing superhero origin film). The world-building alone is impressive; the Afro-futuristic qualities of Wakanda are jaw-dropping! And the story is equally impressive. Top-notch performances really round out the film, particularly from lead Chadwick Boseman as T'Challa, and Michael B. Jordan's Killmonger — arguably one of the best on-screen villains to date. And I love that the film opens in Oakland, a city special to Coogler. The film deserves its praise, and so does Coogler, for delivering a mainstream success without quieting his artistic voice. Marvel Studios , Disney+ Black Panther is available for streaming on Disney+. 8. Black Panther: Wakanda Forever (2022), understandably, was an incredibly difficult film for Coogler to take on. Besides the expected pressure that comes with delivering a sequel to a critically acclaimed film, there came the unexpected death of Chadwick Boseman. It's clear at this point that Coogler is the kind of director who is very involved with his crew, especially his actors, which is why he almost quit filmmaking altogether after Boseman's death. He pushed forward, though, and delivered a film rooted in loss, mourning, and the life that's lived after. The film grossed over $859 million, had the largest November opening weekend ever, and defined Coogler as an auteur able to alchemize real grief into collective meaning. Marvel Studios , Disney+ The film's emotion is felt throughout, though it really shines through Angela Bassett, who plays T'Challa's mother. Coogler really gave her so much to work with, and she in return delivered a powerful performance that rightfully earned her an Academy Award nomination for Best Supporting Actress. Black Panther: Wakanda Forever is available to stream on Disney+. Before we end on his latest film, it's worth mentioning two projects Coogler was involved with outside of directing: Creed III (2023) and the docu-series Hurricane Katrina: Race Against Time (2025). MGM, Lightbox, National Geographic Coogler took on a story and producing credit for Creed III, intentionally stepping aside so Michael B. Jordan could make his directorial debut — it feels like a passing of the torch and testament to Coogler’s growing role as a mentor shaping the next generation of Black storytellers. Coogler was also a producer on Creed II, but was less involved overall due to his commitment to Black Panther, which came out the same year as Creed II. And his involvement as executive producer on Hurricane Katrina: Race Against Time, directed by Traci Curry, is another project that aligns with a theme present throughout his work: the institutions that fail and/or ostracize Black people, and the humanity that persists anyway.Creed III is available to stream on YouTube, Apple TV, and Prime Video. Hurricane Katrina: Race Against Time can be found on Hulu and Disney+. 9. Sinners (2025) is Coogler's most impressive film to date. As soon as he announced he was working on a period film for his next project post-Black Panther, studios scrambled over one another for distribution rights — it ultimately went to Warner Brothers, and as part of the deal, Coogler got full control of the final cut of the movie, a portion of the film's profits upon release (instead of later, on the backend), and ownership of the film after 25 years. That's unheard of! It's the kind of deal only a director with two successful franchises under their belt can get. Sinners released last year to critical acclaim and box office success — it grossed over $369 million and is nominated for 16 Academy Awards at this years Oscars, including Best Picture and Best Director. It's a huge win for Coogler, and more than enough proof that we're witnessing generational talent and a Hollywood legend in real time. Warner Bros. Pictures Coogler's inspiration for the Sinners screenplay was his late uncle who was diagnosed with terminal cancer and died while Coogler was in post-production on Creed. The two were incredibly close and he credits his uncle for introducing him to blues music, a key player in the Sinners' soundscape. He described making Sinners as a "ritual of remembrance," and based on our retrospective, I'd say that's true of every project in his catalogue. Sinners is available for streaming on HBO Max. And there you have it. A career retrospective of a ground-breaking director. Currently, Coogler is working on a reboot of the iconic TV series X-Files for Hulu and the third installment of Black Panther. Beyond that, he's cooking up something for a film genre he hasn't explored yet: rom-coms. Personally, I'm seated for anything Coogler. Because wherever his pen takes him next, his heart, soul, and unmistakable voice will follow.
