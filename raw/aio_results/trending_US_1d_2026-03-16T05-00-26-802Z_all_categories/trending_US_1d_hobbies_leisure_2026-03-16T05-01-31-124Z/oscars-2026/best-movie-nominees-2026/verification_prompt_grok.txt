Now being on the tail end of things, having seen many of the groups that have overlap with Oscar voters making their decisions, it is clear that a “Sinners” surge post-breaking the Oscar nominations record was a bit overhyped, at least on the craft side of things. In addition to winning Best Picture and Best Director at the BAFTAs and the guild awards , “One Battle After Another” has swept just about every major bellwether for Best Adapted Screenplay, Best Cinematography, and Best Editing. Where “Sinners” has been doing better than expected is in the acting categories. The fellow Warner Bros. Pictures release won both Best Ensemble and Best Actor for star Michael B. Jordan at the Actor Awards , and Wunmi Mosaku won Best Supporting Actress at the BAFTA Awards. The Actors Branch is by far the biggest branch in the Academy, with almost double the numbers of some of the next biggest branches like Executives and Marketing and Publicity. As I said in the last edition of “ IndieWire’s The Lead Up ,” we have empirical evidence that actors are very keen on voting for at least one of the “Sinners” stars to win an Oscar , with Jordan being the strongest candidate. Before we get into some What Ifs, I will add that “Sinners” has continually won awards for Best Original Screenplay, Best Casting, and Best Original Score. Those three categories seem unimpeachable, and would result in filmmaker Ryan Coogler receiving his first Academy Award after directing three other Oscar-nominated films, casting maven Francine Maisler earning the first ever competitive Oscar given to someone in her field, and composer Ludwig Göransson becoming a three-time Best Original Score winner (placing him ahead of other all-star composers like Trent Reznor and Atticus Ross, Hans Zimmer, Marvin Hamlisch, and fellow 2026 nominee Alexandre Desplat). If you take away the five categories that “One Battle After Another” and “Sinners” are shoo-ins for (and for the sake of this experiment, I am not waxing poetic about the latter film still having a shot at Best Picture; you fellow hopefuls are seen and heard), that leaves two-thirds more of Oscar categories to consider. Go with God for the shorts categories. I will point out that Netflix has had a strong track record in those categories, so “The Singers” and “All the Empty Rooms” feel like safe picks for Best Live Action Short and Best Documentary Short, respectively. Netflix had also previously been so strong in the Best Documentary Feature category that the Documentary Branch refused to nominate any films from the distributors for a few years, but “The Perfect Neighbor” finally being the one to breakthrough makes it the category frontrunner, even if this season of documentary awards were all over the place. At the end of the day, a lot of people have watched the film. Both “Sentimental Value” and “The Secret Agent” were big winners at Cannes that were distributed in the United States by Neon, and are now both nominated for Best International Feature as well as Best Picture. “Sentimental Value” is favored to win, as voters often mirror how BAFTA voted, plus the Joachim Trier film received nine nominations. But because the films also made it into the acting categories, after having their stars win at the Golden Globes , doubt has been cast on what categories, if any, either Neon film is sure to win. BAFTA really threw a wrench into that because it has a lot more overlap with how the Academy votes than the Golden Globes does. It was there that “One Battle After Another” star Sean Penn won Best Supporting Actor over “Sentimental Value” star Stellan Skarsgård. It was also there that “Sentimental Value” won the equivalent of Best International Feature, while “The Secret Agent” won Best International Feature at the Golden Globes. All this to say that if the only categories that “Sentimental Value” is competitive in are Best Supporting Actor and Best International Feature, the least likely scenario seems to be the film winning both. Regardless of the fact that he was not nominated against fellow contenders Skarsgård and “Sinners” star Delroy Lindo, the safest bet would be to predict Penn wins Best Supporting Actor based on the two-time Oscar winner triumphing in that category at the Actor Awards. That leaves “The Secret Agent” with nothing, despite also being nominated for Best Picture, Best Actor, and Best Casting. Again, “Sinners” has the edge in the latter two categories. Finally, there is Best Actress, arguably the easiest category to predict, with “Hamnet” star Jessie Buckley liking becoming the sole Oscar winner for the Focus Features. And then Best Supporting Actress, one of the hardest to predict, is led by another triumvirate similar to the Best Supporting Actor race. Though “One Battle After Another” star Teyana Taylor won the Golden Globe, her momentum seems to have petered out since then. Critical darling Amy Madigan has both a Critics Choice Award and an Actor Award for her supporting performance in “Weapons,” but unlike recent comp Jamie Lee Curtis, her horror film was not nominated anywhere else. Like Penn, she seemingly has the support of the Actors branch, but based on how many nominations it got, scores more voters watched “Sinners,” hence why BAFTA winner Wunmi Mosaku feels like a safer choice to be the predicted winner. After what seems like a full year of talking about these films, the time has come to make predictions for the winners of the 98th Oscars. For a while, on paper at least, it has appeared to be a fairly easy year to predict. Virtually all the endless precursor awards settled on One Battle After Another until Sinners shook up the race at the very last minute (halfway through actual Oscar voting) at last week’s Actor Awards, and suddenly some were declaring the race had opened up. Other than Best Actress, the acting races also appeared fluid going into final balloting. RELATED: Oscar Nominations 2026: ‘Sinners’ Leads With Record 16, ‘One Battle’ 13 But the real wild card, as I have been writing, is a new Academy stipulation that voters must attest they have seen all the nominees in a category before voting. Although this is largely on an honesty basis, there are technical additions to the ballot that may have complicated the process for voters. You have to click that you have seen a film if not already given credit on the Academy’s digital screening room. RELATED: How To Watch The Oscars Online & On TV Anytime there is new technology there can be problems, and I have heard for some there were. One voter who first chose to vote for International Feature Film told me their entire ballot shut down with a “Thank you for voting.” Calling the helpline, the Academy member apologized for bothering and said the IT person told them they weren’t alone, and the helpline has been very “busy” with others experiencing similar problems as well as other glitches. This voter had to get their ballot restored in order to vote in the remainder of the categories and told me they never could have figured it out on their own. In the end, bugs and all, everyone wanting to vote got to vote. RELATED: Where To Watch The 2026 Oscar Contenders, From ‘Sinners’ To ‘Sentimental Value’ Still, the new rule requiring “proof” or at least personal attestment to watching the films clearly has been intimidating for some, adding more pressure than usual, based on my informal surveys, and perhaps resulting in a smaller number of voters this year among the 10,000-plus eligible pool. The Academy never comments on numbers, but at a dinner the other night with a couple of members, one stunned me by saying they weren’t voting at all. This member had always dutifully participated for decades, even been involved in the campaigns, but this year confessed they just hadn’t seen enough so decided to skip it entirely rather than just vote for what they had seen. The other member though had watched everything, even the shorts, and was a model voter. I talked with a few who even mentioned the names of films they were running out of time to see; Hamnet and Sentimental Value came up more than once. [R5] Who will win (and who should win) at the 2026 Oscars

We did it, everyone. We made it to the end of Oscar season. I’m not saying this awards season was too long and grueling, but I’m just saying that I believe Stephen King used it as the inspiration for “The Long Walk” – complete with everyone kinda just wanting to be put out of their misery at some point. (I’m sure the BBC and BAFTAs in particular feel that way considering whatever the heck THAT whole situation was. It was more than a travesty. It was more than a debacle. It was a trabacle.)This sense of weary exhaustion at the finish line is a shame because, thanks to this needlessly long march, we’ve lost sight of how fun and good this year’s Academy Awards are. No one’s trying to ruin the show by eliminating categories, insulting nominees, and deleting hosts. There’s not really a true villain movie that people are dreading to see win – “Hamnet” is your closest culprit there, but even that’s viewed as more quaint than sinister – and there’s no nasty narratives infesting the show. Not even a single Karla Sofia Gascon tweet! (Wait, she posted something about the Timothee Chalamet “controversy”? Dammit, we were so close!) And best of all … basically all the movies are good! A year where the worst notable nominees are “F1: The Movie” and “Hamnet” is a year worth celebrating indeed. And when it comes to the main two front-runners, for cinephiles, studio heads, and average filmgoers alike, it’s the rare opposite of an “Alien vs. Predator” scenario: Whoever wins, we win.Indeed, just move the ceremony date up to not a quarter of the way into a whole new year, and the vibes would be terrific going into Oscar night. The box office is back! Hollywood made good movies! Everything’s fixed and nothing bad is on the horizon! Now, to take a quick sip of coffee and see what’s new with the iconic major studio behind the two Best Picture favorites … Well, let’s just politely ignore that existential nightmare that makes “Sirat” seem like a new Paddington movie and focus instead on the Oscar race – which, in a rarity, is actually still a race across most of the categories. Thanks to the rise in precursor attention these days, normally things are pretty locked into place, and it’s a march to the inevitable – but chaos reigns in 2026, and the Academy Awards are no different. So let’s make a misguided attempt to predict the chaos and make my annual guesses for who will be crowned come Sunday night.Will win: “Sinners”Should win: “Sinners”Let’s start with the nominees who can happily hit the open bar without fear of a public speaking part on Sunday night. As the obvious final nominee to crack the ten, the cliche but crowd-pleasing “F1: The Movie” is just happy to be there along with “The Secret Agent” and “Sentimental Value,” the two foreign language successes that never quite reached that top-tier level of buzz and acclaim to transcend voters’ language barriers and pull a “Parasite.” Their real Best Picture race will be in Best International Film. Even if “Bugonia” wasn’t too weird and divisive, the dark conspiracy drama feels like it came out too long ago – but then again, everything about this year’s Oscars feels like forever ago. “Marty Supreme” is too manic, while “Train Dreams” is too small and quiet – and the Netflix of it all makes it feel even smaller and quieter. And speaking of Netflix, “Frankenstein” … well, “Frankenstein” just isn’t good enough, especially when Guillermo del Toro already won Best Picture for a more universally lauded genre movie not all that long ago. Then there’s “Hamnet,” the closest thing this year has to a villain thanks to its old-school Oscar prestige energy and emotional insistence. Even though it’s the least of the nominees for me, it certainly has its fans – so much so there was a brief moment where I genuinely thought it might sneak through a split vote between the cool kid movies “Sinners” and “One Battle After Another” to claim a shocker victory. Much like “Frankenstein,” though, Chloe Zhao’s won recently for better – and more important, the precursor receipts just aren’t there. Other than the Golden Globes, voted on by non-Academy members, it just hasn’t won much anywhere else. I stuck to the old Academy rules last year and went with the safe respectable handsome drama for adults (“Conclave”) over the young, thorny, edgy choice (“Anora”), and I was made into a fool. A FOOL! AND I SHANT BE FOOLED AGAIN! A For Your Consideration campaign for the Ice Cube “War of the Worlds” would’ve been less embarrassing.That leaves “Sinners” and “One Battle After Another,” one of the most charming head-to-head races we’ve had in a long time. Normally, the top two competitors for Best Picture are diametric opposites of each other, narrative counterpoints: the traditional “Conclave” versus the new school “Anora,” the small gritty indie “The Hurt Locker” versus the ultimate shiny blockbuster “Avatar,” the musical Hollywood fantasy of “La La Land” versus the grounded human drama “Moonlight,” the heavy war film “Saving Private Ryan” versus the light costume dramedy “Shakespeare in Love.” These two, though, are shockingly the exact same: They’re two risky non-IP movies for grown-ups, blending clear political and social commentary with genre-mashing big-screen entertainment, miraculously shepherded through corporate Hollywood by two auteur writer-directors taking big risky creative swings for more money than studios would likely prefer. And they both paid off, with success at the box office (“Sinners” more so than “OBAA,” but a $200 million worldwide gross from the director of “Inherent Vice” is a victory in its own right), in critics reviews, in the cultural eye, and now with the little gold men. They’re both the kind of large-scale Hollywood success story – on both a creative and financial level – the Academy wants to celebrate. They even come from the same studio: Warner Bros., or at least the last gasp of it. They’re so similar, I can’t help but think Sunday night will result in a split ballot, with the Academy wanting to shine a spotlight on both and therefore giving one movie its director prize and Best Picture to the other. When it comes to the Best Picture half of this split, all the logic points to “One Battle After Another.” The revolutionary dramedy has won a vast majority of the precursor awards, from the critics prizes to most of the guild trophies and the BAFTA (British Oscars) Best Picture. It has all the narratives – a political statement, a Hollywood success story, a writer-director getting long-deserved shine – while not feeling to voters like the boring or obvious pick. Meanwhile, we don’t talk enough about how strange this “Sinners” run is. A bloody, sexy, unapologetic genre movie released closer to last year’s Oscars than this year’s ceremony? That’s a collection of all the things that traditionally DON’T win Academy Awards, much less get nominated – and I know we’re in a post-Best Picture nominee “The Substance”/hot dog hands in a Best Picture winner world, but the old Academy is still in there somewhere. But I’ll be damned just like the vampires: I can’t help going with “Sinners.”“One Battle After Another” may have won most of the precursors, but “Sinners” won the most recent notable one: Best Ensemble at the Actor Awards (formerly the SAG Awards, a rebranding that makes a lot of sense but also I hate it) which is also the largest branch of the Academy. And the enthusiasm in the room came through my streaming screen. Thanks to that victory, Ryan Coogler’s film has the energy going into this final stretch – but it also had momentum at the start. After all, “Sinners” didn’t just get nominations: It got the MOST nominations, ever, with 16. Highly nominated movies can certainly have lowly trophy counts come the end of Oscar night; it feels like Scorsese’s last decade of movies all had handfuls of nominations only to result in empty hands, for instance. But breaking an almost century-long record – especially for such an unconventional Academy favorite, a spring-released horror movie – isn’t something that happens on accident without passion behind it. And considering Best Picture is decided by a preferential ballot, meaning the most beloved movie will fare better than the most merely liked, that passion literally counts for something.“Sinners” has defied the odds at every phase of its existence thus far – why not one more time? Will win: Michael B. Jordan, “Sinners”Should win: Timothee Chalamet, “Marty Supreme”I’m still at the energized phase of overwriting these picks, so let’s break all the options down here! Ethan Hawke’s certainly due for a win one of these years – and this fantastically verbose, practically one-man-show Lorenz Hart portrait would be a worthy way to earn it – but “Blue Moon” is probably too small and too outside of the general conversation to pull off the surprise. Wagner Maura would be worthy too, but “The Secret Agent” began to fly under the radar since nomination morning, and foreign language acting winners are rare. In Best Actor, it’s happened only once with Roberto Benigni – and I think the Academy’s still recovering from that. Elsewhere, it was hard enough to get Leonardo DiCaprio his first Oscar – they’re not giving him a second one this soon, and certainly not for one closer to goofball mode. This Leo talks brings us to Leo Jr., aka Timmy Chalamet. He’s great in “Marty Supreme,” a true movie star performance that made a period piece ping-pong dramedy a Christmas smash and that would be wholly intolerable if it didn’t balance the character’s chaos and charisma just right. But the Academy sure loves to make actors – especially the young ones – wait for their wins. And wait. And wait. And wait some more before they win for “The Revenant” or “Crazy Heart” or “Scent of a Woman” or some other performance we tolerate more than totally love. That was probably already Timmy’s fate – but then he fully clinched it when he gave his SAG speech last year about how he wants the awards and wants to be recognized as one of the greats. And if there’s one thing Academy voters love more than making actors wait, it’s making actors who REALLY want an Oscar REALLY wait. (Just ask Glenn Close.) Cue Timmy getting passed over last year – even for the good version of the kind of Oscar bait biopic performance that usually wins the long-delayed trophy 20 years from now – and cue Timmy getting passed over by both the BAFTAs and Screen Actors Guild after a promising campaign start. And that was all before … and I can’t believe I have to bring this up … Timmy Hates Ballet and Opera-Gate. I’m shocked, just shocked, that the internet is debating a famous person's inarticulate-but-accurate thoughts based on a quote being willfully pulled out of context. Doesn’t seem like the internet at all. It’s one of our dumber controversies – but unfortunately for Timmy, this excuse to be resistant broke out JUUUUUUUST in time for the Oscar voting window. Best of all, if voters – for rational or irrational reasons – find voting for Timmy harder to swallow than the garlic in “Sinners,” they have another quality place to redirect their vote: Michael B. Jordan. It’s a similarly compelling movie star performance – times two – in a surprise smash riding on his leading man shoulders, combined with a similarly compelling “crowning the next generation” awards narrative. And while a Timmy win feels inevitable to some, a Michael B. Jordan win feels invigorating – at least that was certainly the vibe when he won at the Actor Awards, a real pop in the crowd coming through. The Academy is obviously not above voting on vibes. Chalamet could certainly still win – like most of these categories, it’s excitingly still a contest after all this time – but with that SAG triumph, Jordan has the positive energy going into Sunday night. Meanwhile the only positive developments surrounding Timmy right now are record highs in affection for the ballet and opera arts. Will win: Jessie Buckley, “Hamnet”Should win: Rose Byrne, “If I Had Legs, I’d Kick You”Listen, they can’t all be fun, exciting, and unpredictable. Jessie Buckley’s been the front-runner for Best Actress since the start of Oscar season, and despite the best efforts of “The Bride!”, that’s how things have stayed. Byrne’s great – and overdue for the recognition – but A24 barely gave “If I Had Legs, I’d Kick You” a real theatrical release much less a real campaign push. Emma Stone’s already won twice – all within the past ten years – and I doubt the Academy will graduate her to Meryl status in barely a decade’s time. Renate Reinsve’s “Sentimental Value” performance is quietly emotional and nuanced, but voters don’t usually do quiet and nuanced for their performance winners. And even though saying this puts my Wisconsin citizenship at risk, Hudson’s not winning for “Song Sung Blue” either. Her comeback narrative is fun, and she genuinely pops in a way she hasn’t in years, but voters aren’t thinking of single-nominee “Song Sung Blue” this late in the game, nor do I think they have the stomach to give a midwestern Neil Diamond cover band biopic one of its top prizes. It all just gives me Best Actor the year of “The Revenant” vibes, where everyone knew Leo was finally winning so everyone else kinda didn’t waste their time. Or that weird year where everyone just decided Renee Zellweger was winning again for a Judy Garland biopic that no one really liked or bothered thinking about again.But also, Buckley’s a steamroller because it’s exactly the kind of performance and narrative that becomes an Oscar steamroller. It’s a loudly emotional performance – the kind that its detractors would say is Most Acting rather than Best Acting – filled with big showstopper Oscar clip moments from an actress who’s been putting in quality work for a while and just needed the right showcase to reach this point. And while much has changed with the Academy in recent years, they haven’t changed THAT much that a grandiose howling performance with big cry moments in a literary-minded prestige costume drama isn’t EXACTLY that kind of role. I mean, “Hamnet” still got eight nominations. It may feel like an also-ran at this point but voters clearly appreciated it – and this is their place to recognize it. So congrats to Buckley – and in a rare situation, she can actually choose whether she wants the trophy or if she wants every DCP print of “The Bride!” deleted and melted out of existence. Tough choice! Will win: Sean Penn, “One Battle After Another”Should win: Benicio Del Toro, “One Battle After Another”At the start of this Oscar season, approximately around the start of the War of 1812, I thought this category was a total toss-up. Penn and Del Toro were both early favorites, but two contenders from the same movie risk splitting the vote – plus they both already have Oscars. Even those lukewarm on “Frankenstein” love Elordi’s physical and sensitive take on the monster, but again, the Academy tends to take its time to reward hot young actors on the rise. Giving Stellan Skarsgard the prize would be a great opportunity to both celebrate a respected acting vet and to spread the love and give “Sentimental Value” a win somewhere – but the buzz since his Golden Globes success died down fast. And Delroy Lindo’s surprise nomination seemed like the win at first, but as “Sinners” has picked up steam, so has his overdue narrative – even though it’s a fairly minor part. So it’s anybody’s game!But then, as the season went along, I realized: Oh, no it’s not. Of course Sean Penn’s winning. Sean Penn was always winning. A big, showy, tic-happy (in a good way) villain performance in the Best Picture frontrunner?
