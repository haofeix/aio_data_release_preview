Whether you’re just starting to consider career options or you’re working toward a specific profession, earning a Bachelor of Arts (BA) degree could help you reach your goals. So what is a Bachelor of Arts degree, and how can it help you build the future you want? A BA degree is a four-year undergraduate liberal arts program that offers a broad-based education and teaches key soft skills — often referred to as interpersonal or people skills. From education and psychology to human services and creative writing, there are BA degree programs that can help you prepare for diverse careers across many industries. If you’re considering an undergraduate degree, it’s important to understand what programs are available, how to earn a Bachelor of Arts degree and the long-term value of a BA degree. What is a Bachelor of Arts Degree All About? Earning a Bachelor of Arts degree can give you a strong foundation of learning across many subjects and help you develop the career skills employers want. BA degrees typically consist of about 40 courses, though the actual number of courses may vary depending on your degree program. Credits are earned through a combination of general education courses, program-specific courses and elective courses. While program-specific and elective courses help expand your knowledge base in a particular field, general education courses lay the foundation for valuable soft skills like creativity, communication and critical thinking. Tramon McZeal ’25 So what's more important, program-specific courses or general education? According to Tramon McZeal ’25, a graduate of the BA in Sociology program at Southern New Hampshire University (SNHU), both can help you with your goals. "Everything I learned was beneficial," he said. "Through each term, I became a better human being." From the day he began his program to graduation day, McZeal said he's gained a greater self-awareness and a better understanding of what he needs to do to achieve success. The focus on soft skills is one of the key features that sets a Bachelor of Arts degree apart from the other primary type of bachelor’s degree — a Bachelor of Science (BS) degree. These soft skills can help you prepare for more advanced degrees and give you the flexibility to navigate an evolving job market. They're also in high demand across many industries and could help you succeed in your chosen career.* According to a 2024 LinkedIn study, 9 out of 10 global executives say that soft skills are more important than ever. LinkedIn data found that some of the most in-demand skills for 2025 include conflict mitigation, adaptability, innovative thinking and public speaking. What's the Difference Between a BA and BS Degree? While both degree types include a foundation of general education courses and program-specific courses, there are some differences between a BA and a BS degree. So, which is a better degree, Bachelor of Arts or Bachelor of Science? It really depends on your interests and career goals. A BA degree explores topics that require critical thinking and research, such as literature, arts or history. BA programs can prepare you for specific careers while also laying a solid foundation in soft skills that can be applied to a variety of career opportunities. A BS degree, on the other hand, typically focuses on more concrete, technical skills and places a bigger focus on program-specific courses. As a result, students with a very specific career in mind may gravitate toward a BS degree, such as an online computer science degree for someone who wants to work as a computer programmer. One of the most significant differences between BA and BS degrees, however, comes down to the programs of study available within each degree type. What Majors Fall Under Bachelor of Arts? Whether you dream of spending your days writing novels or you want to focus on helping others, there’s a BA degree program for you. Are creative pursuits your passion? While a Bachelor of Arts degree does differ from a Bachelor of Fine Arts degree, there are still many program options that can help you turn your love of the arts into a career. Do you want to make a difference in your community? A BA degree could also help you land jobs in social services, nonprofit work or education. Still not sure what you want to do with your career? Explore some of the most common types of BA degrees available — and the jobs they can help you get — to see what could be a good fit. Bachelor of Arts in Communication When you earn a communication degree, you can learn the skills you need to work in journalism, social media, public relations, advertising and other related fields. You’ll study how to convey ideas effectively to various audiences and have the opportunity to apply your learning to real-world situations. Within a communication program, you could focus your studies on areas like business communication, new media, professional writing and public relations. This type of Bachelor of Arts degree could help you prepare for a variety of jobs, including: Public Relations (PR) Specialist As a PR specialist, you could help create and maintain a positive reputation for an organization or individual client by writing media releases, developing social media programs and coordinating with the media. According to BLS, advertising, promotion and marketing managers earned a median salary of $159,660 in 2024, and jobs in the field are projected to grow 6% from 2024 to 2034.* Learn more about what you can do with a communication degree. Bachelor of Arts in Education With a degree in education, you can prepare for teacher licensure and gain the skills you need to work as a classroom teacher or other educator. What Does It Take to Earn a Bachelor of Arts Degree? Once you’ve chosen a major in your Bachelor of Arts program, it’s important to explore how to get started with a BA and what it takes to graduate with your degree. What is Required to Start a BA Program? While admission procedures vary, most colleges and universities have a few core requirements for entering a bachelor’s degree program. Typically, the process begins with an application. Once you’ve applied, you’ll likely be required to supply a transcript from your high school or GED program, as well as any other schools you’ve attended. If you need financial aid, you’ll want to complete the current Free Application for Federal Student Aid (FAFSA). Some schools may also require SAT or other standardized tests for admission, while others, such as SNHU, do not. If you’ve completed any college courses already through an associate degree program or past bachelor’s degree courses, you may be able to transfer those credits toward your new degree program. At SNHU, for example, six 8-week terms throughout the year mean you can complete as many as 12 courses annually. If you take a full course load and transfer credits, you could finish your degree faster. If you take fewer classes each term, it may take longer than four years to complete your program. How Much Does It Cost to Earn a BA Degree? The cost of a Bachelor of Arts degree can vary significantly depending on tuition and other fees. But even in an online degree program, there can be additional costs aside from your tuition — such as textbooks, online learning resources, software and other technology needs. What is a Bachelor of Arts Degree Able to Do for Your Career? One of the most important things to consider when exploring a Bachelor of Arts degree is what it can do for you, your career and your life. When it comes to a BA degree, there's a lot of potential.
