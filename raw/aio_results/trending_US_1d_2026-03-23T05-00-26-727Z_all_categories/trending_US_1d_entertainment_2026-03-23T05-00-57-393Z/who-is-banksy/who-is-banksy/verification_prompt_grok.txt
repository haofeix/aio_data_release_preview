Banksy is one of the biggest attractions that contemporary art has to offer today. The British street artist captivates audiences worldwide with his graffiti art, leaving his artistic mark in numerous countries. But who is Banksy really? His trademark and a significant factor in his success is his anonymity. For over 20 years, the art world has been puzzling over who might be hiding behind the pseudonym. In any case, Banksy is best understood as a one-man art project. His concept combines a mixture of street art with a simple yet striking visual language, guerrilla tactics, political messages, and a rejection of the art market. This fascinates and polarises the public in equal measure. However, it is undisputed that his works have significantly influenced 21st-century painting and inspired many imitators. Banksy's Career Began Around the Year 2000 Given Banksy's consistently secret identity, there is also no confirmed information about his biography or artistic development. It is said that he was born in either 1973 or 1974 in Bristol, where he grew up. He is believed to have learnt the art of graffiti there and started his career as a street artist. Beyond that, little is known about him. The question "Who is Banksy?" can really only be answered through his artistic activities. The art phantom Banksy first came to public attention around the turn of the millennium. As early as 1999, he is said to have shown his works in self-organised exhibitions. In the early 2000s, he began making a name for himself with his graffiti and stencil works. Over the years, his iconic works began appearing on public buildings in various countries around the world. Banksy's anonymity is undoubtedly an important pillar of his popularity. As is often the case in the art world, Banksy's great success can largely be attributed to psychology. The idea of a phantom sneaking around the houses at night, leaving illegal murals behind, exerts a great fascination. The audience loves the game of hide-and-seek and the mystery surrounding his true identity. Almost everyone has their own theory about who Banksy might be. If Banksy's identity were revealed, he would risk a lot. He would lose his mysterious aura and become just one of many artists. Unauthorised exhibitions showcasing reproductions of Banksy's art are held worldwide. The concept of working under a pseudonym is not new in the world of art and culture. Banksy has adopted this principle and does his utmost to maintain the game of hide-and-seek for as long as possible. The question "Who is Banksy?" will likely intrigue the art world for a while. It is not only Banksy who benefits from his anonymity. Art collectors and investors who have spent large sums on his works also have an interest in him remaining unrecognised. As long as Banksy exists only as a phantom, the art world retains one of its most interesting - and not least financially lucrative - figures.
