Styling Your Bob Haircut For a Sleek Look To achieve a smooth and sleek bob, use a straightening iron to smooth your hair, section by section. Follow with a smoothing serum to tame flyaways and add shine – apply it sparingly from mid-lengths to ends to avoid weighing the hair down. Finish with a light mist of shine spray or anti-humidity spray for a glossy, long-lasting hold. For Textured Bobs To add natural body and texture to your bob, apply a texturising spray or lightweight mousse to damp hair before blow-drying. Focus on the roots for lift and through the mid-lengths for soft movement. A sea salt spray is perfect for a tousled, lived-in look, while a volumising mousse can give fine hair extra bounce and fullness. For a Voluminous Bob For added fullness and body, apply a light volumising mousse to damp hair, focusing on the roots. Then, blow-dry using a large round brush, lifting the hair at the roots to encourage height and curling in slightly at the ends for a smooth, voluminous finish. This technique adds natural lift and helps create a polished, salon-quality look. Tips for Maintaining a Bob Haircut Regular Trims To keep your bob looking sharp and healthy, it’s essential to visit the salon for a trim every 6–8 weeks. Regular trims help maintain the shape and structure of the cut while preventing split ends from traveling up the hair shaft. This keeps your style fresh and your hair in its best condition. Hair Care Routine It’s vital to use a nourishing shampoo and conditioner to keep your hair hydrated and healthy – especially if you have thicker or coloured hair. These formulas help replenish moisture, enhance manageability, and protect against damage. Avoiding Flatness Blow-dry your hair to boost volume, focusing on lift at the roots for a much fuller look. This polished, shoulder-grazing look is exactly right. “This is a collarbone to chest-length haircut that sits between a bob and long hair,” says Brenner. “[It’s] incredibly versatile.”A mid-length cut works on any texture, but you’ll want to give it a little movement and ooh-la-la to make it look intentional, not like you’re just waiting for last year’s bob to grow out. “Imagine hair that swings when you walk,” says Brenner. To achieve that, she recommends, ask for some internal shaping (cutting layers underneath the top layer to add volume) and a “blunt but soft” perimeter.Waterfall hairGetty ImagesGetty ImagesGetty ImagesGetty ImagesGetty ImagesIf you crash-landed on a lush, tropical island, and decided you’d rather stay and become queen than try to go home, you’d need the right hair for the job. The clear answer here is long, waist-length hair that cascades down your back in silky ripples—just like the waterfall you now live behind. Obviously.Of course, it’s not very practical (or ethical) to rule your own island, but the fantasy is really all about the hair anyway. “The cut is blunt with a clean center part and long lengths reaching mid-back,” says Chicago-based hairstylist Amy Abramite, who likes to think of these satin-finish waves as a cooler alternative to bouncy Hollywood hair. “Ask for a long, one-length cut with no layers, styled with a crimping waver.”Soft sculpted bobGetty ImagesGetty ImagesGetty ImagesGetty ImagesGetty ImagesIf a bob could be hung in the Musée d’Orsay—somewhere between the Degas ballerinas and Rodin’s Thinker—it would look a lot like this: soft, impressionistic, but meticulously sculpted.Best for straight, fine, or wavy textures, this cut is a masterpiece of proportions.“[Ask for] a bob that feels clean and modern, but still soft and movable,” says Milwaukee-based hairstylist Loren Kramer. “Not too blunt at the ends.”Those with denser, curlier hair can get the look by asking for “a rounded curl bob with layers that release weight and build shape,” says Los Angeles-based hairstylist Dusty Schlabach. “The curls build a soft, dome-like silhouette. Shorter where they need lift, longer where they need weight.”Spring hair colorsHamptons blondeGetty ImagesGetty ImagesGetty ImagesThere are the beach blondes in it for salty, sandy waves; there are the polished Hollywood blondes, perhaps the truest, blondest blondes; and then there are the Hamptons blondes, who evoke visions of convertibles and seasonal homes.A Hamptons blonde shade is as beautiful and dimensional as a Martha Stewart tablescape. The look reads creamy, buttery blonde, but gets a deep richness from honey undertones. “It feels sunlit and soft, but never icy or overly bright,” says New York City-based hairstylist Rogerio Cavalcante. Ask for a warm blonde with golden undertones.And if you’re still sporting a rooty winter blonde, ask your stylist for “a quick lift that will give your natural color a golden boost,” says New York City-based colorist Tiffanie Richards.Dimensional cocoaGetty ImagesGetty ImagesGetty ImagesGetty ImagesYou can keep your well-manicured hedges and your Dutch colonial. Dimensional cocoa is a sleek, stylish brunette for those who prefer to conjure spring in Milan or Copenhagen. “At first glance, it reads as one color, but when the hair moves, you see slight shifts of light reflection,” says New York City-based colorist Lena Ott. “It feels soft and expensive, not flat or opaque.”Adds Cavalcante, you’re aiming for a deep, glossy brunette with cocoa or espresso undertones. “This color feels elevated and timeless, without the upkeep of heavy highlighting,” he says. Just be sure to ask for a gloss to give it that extra-polished finish—a key element of looking posh and mysterious while shoe-shopping along Corso Vittorio.Strawberry beigeGetty ImagesGetty ImagesGetty ImagesGetty ImagesLike a glass of Veuve Clicquot garnished with berries, this strawberry-beige color straddles the line between upscale polish and whimsy. Yes, this look has been seen at Chateau Marmont recently, but the wearer is certainly not too formal for a midnight skinny-dip.“This is a muted, earthy copper. Soft gold mixed with peach and a hint of rose,” says Kramer. The color should feel natural and worn-in even if it’s not, in fact, your natural color. Ask for a warm base with copper and gold toning, she adds: “The balance is everything…. Too red and it’s loud, too gold and it loses depth.”Desert caramelGetty ImagesGetty ImagesGetty ImagesGetty ImagesWhoever told you that you can’t have everything was not a desert-caramel bronde. You can indeed have the brightness of blonde with the ease of a natural rooty beige! (That’s what they were talking about when they said “everything,” right?)“Clients want a low-maintenance color that still feels polished,” says New York City-based colorist Elisabeth Leary. This low-key sandy shade checks the boxes: effortless, cool, and minimal salon time.“It’s about enhancement, not transformation,” says Leary, who recommends keeping it close to your natural shade, lifting only one or two levels if you’re starting darker. Wait … back to side part.For all the group-chat discourse, TikTok hot takes and millennial existential crises that the hair-parting pendulum spurred, it overlooked a major truth: that wearing your hair down doesn’t need to be such a dichotomy. A lot of us stick to our go-to look day after day — so much so that the suggestion to shift our part by a couple inches feels radical. But what if this spring, instead of debating which part is back “in,” we just … switched up our hair style regularly?To be fair, there’s a reason we find ourselves locked in on a signature look: “If you’ve got a set schedule and you know you can do the same old, same old in 25 minutes and it will look the way you expect it to, there isn’t great motivation to try something new,” says Toronto-based hairstylist Ashley Readings.Aside from the good ol’ fun of experimenting with hairstyles (like the joy of wearing a new outfit, but without the hit to your credit card bill), there’s a protective factor too. “A lot of the damage our hair suffers is from repetition,” says Readings. “Many people struggle to see hair growth around their face-framing layers because they focus their hot tools on those pieces and if they don’t change their part or style, they get damaged more than the rest.” ARTICLE CONTINUES BELOW To get you started, we put together a week’s worth of different ways to wear your hair down, with tips from Readings to get it done easily and quickly. Bella Hadid, Gillian Anderson. Getty Images A Throwback HeadbandThe look: With “Love Story” spurring a Carolyn Bessette Kennedy style revival, we’re clamouring for tortoiseshell headbands again. Miu Miu’s fall 2026 runway also made a strong case for the stretchy comb ones from our grade-school days (a look so good Gillian Anderson wore it down the catwalk then kept it on even after the show). “Headbands are a saviour when you want to extend a blow dry, suffer from greasy roots or are leaving a workout class,” says Readings. “You can push it back out of your face and still benefit from the style in the lengths of your hair, whether that means heat-styled strands or natural texture.”The effort level: Zero.The styling tip: If you have short hair and a headband that goes all the way around, it might cause the strands at the nape of your neck to stick up awkwardly. Readings recommends pushing it away from your neck, and then using a tail comb to pull out those pieces so they sit under the band instead. “The classic headband that only sits on the top of the head requires no tips — they are that easy.”The tools: Kitsch Tort Wide Headband, $20, mykitsch.ca. Getty Images The Tight Ear-TuckThe look: This ultra-flattering look (hello, cheekbones) is basically just hair worn down and side-parted, but with extra asymmetry thanks to the sleekness on one side. Even better: it works with any length and style — blown out, waved, curled or air-dried.The effort level: Got an extra five minutes?The styling tip: You want this to look a little sharper than simply tucking one side behind your ear. Readings recommends using gel and an edge comb to smooth hair down, and using a tiny elastic to tie the “tucked” section to hair back near the nape of your neck. It will be hidden by the rest of your hair that falls overtop. How to Choose the Best Hairstyle for Straight Hair Choosing the right straight hairstyle is easier when you follow these steps based on hair type, face shape, and length. By Hair Type Straight hair often looks smooth and flat, so choose hairstyles with layers or texture. Light layering, textured tops, or soft fringes help add movement, reduce flatness, and make straight hair look fuller and more stylish. By Face Shape Face shape matters when choosing straight hairstyles. Round faces look better with height on top, square faces suit side parts, long faces need width, and oval faces work with most clean or textured styles. By Hair Length Hair length changes how straight hair looks and behaves. Short styles need texture to avoid stiffness, medium length adds movement, and longer straight hair works best with layers to prevent it from looking flat or heavy. How to Ask Your Barber for a Straight Haircut Use these simple steps to explain your straight haircut clearly, so your barber understands your style, length, and overall look. Explain the Style You Want Tell your barber how you want your straight hair to look. Say if you prefer a clean, textured, or natural style. Mention if you style your hair daily or want a low-maintenance haircut that stays neat. Show a Reference Photo Bring one or two photos of straight haircuts you like. Photos help your barber understand the exact shape, top length, and finish you want. This avoids confusion and helps match the style to your hair type. Confirm the Length and Fade Clearly explain the length you want on top and sides. Ask for short, medium, or long length and mention fade type like low or mid fade. This helps your barber create a balanced, clean straight haircut. How to Style Straight Hair for Men Follow these simple steps to style straight hair easily. Follow with a hydrating conditioner, then towel-dry until damp. Clean, prepped hair styles better and keeps straight hair smooth and manageable. Add Volume at the Roots Straight hair often looks flat, so add lift at the roots. Apply a small amount of Sea Salt Spray to damp hair and gently lift with your fingers. This adds volume, texture, and makes straight hair look fuller. Blow-Dry for Shape Use a blow dryer on medium heat to shape your hair. Lift the roots while drying and guide hair into place. Blow-drying helps set volume, control direction, and makes straight hairstyles last longer. Finish with a Styling Product Finish with a small amount of Texture Clay or Styling Cream. Rub it between your hands and apply lightly for control and definition. These products keep straight hair neat, flexible, and natural without stiffness. How to Maintain Straight Haircuts Maintaining straight haircuts is easy when you follow these simple care and grooming steps. Get Regular Trims Get a trim every 4-6 weeks to keep straight haircuts sharp and tidy. Regular trims remove split ends, keep the shape clean, and stop straight hair from looking flat or messy as it grows out. Keep Hair Healthy Healthy hair makes straight hairstyles look better. Wash hair 2-3 times a week, use a hydrating conditioner, and avoid rough towel drying. A small amount of Forte Series Hydrating Argan Oil helps keep hair smooth, soft, and easy to manage. Avoid Heavy Products Heavy products can weigh straight hair down and make it look greasy. Use lightweight options like Forte Series Styling Cream or Texture Clay to add control and shape without killing volume or natural movement.
