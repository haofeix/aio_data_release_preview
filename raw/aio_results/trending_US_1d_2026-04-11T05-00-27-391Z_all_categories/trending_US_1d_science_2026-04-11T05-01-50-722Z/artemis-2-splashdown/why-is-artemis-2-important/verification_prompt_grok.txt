[R5] What is Artemis II?
(https://airandspace.si.edu/stories/editorial/what-artemis-ii)
"""
Soon, four astronauts will launch on a mission to the vicinity of the Moon for the first time in over 50 years. The mission, called Artemis II, is the second mission — and first crewed mission — of NASA’s Artemis program. NASA is currently targeting Wednesday, April 1, 2026 for the launch. The April 1 launch window opens at 6:24 pm and lasts 120 minutes. NASA has also identified additional launch windows between April 2 and April 6.Here’s what to expect from the Artemis II mission. This banner hanging on a fence at Launch Complex 39B was signed by NASA employees and contractors involved in the Artemis II mission. The 10-day Artemis II mission will fly around the Moon and is considered a test flight of NASA’s Exploration Ground Systems (the systems involved in launching and recovery rockets and spacecraft), the SLS rocket, and the Orion spacecraft.As a follow-up mission to the uncrewed Artemis I mission in 2022, this crewed mission will allow NASA to flight test Orion’s life-support systems with astronauts on board. This is essential as NASA intends to send astronauts on longer-duration missions as the Artemis program goes on.Who is the Artemis II Crew?Unlike the Apollo program, which launched crews of three, the Artemis program will feature a crew of four astronauts: commander Reid Wiseman, pilot Victor Glover, mission specialist Christina Koch, and mission specialist Jeremy Hansen. This historic crew features many "firsts." Click on their names to learn more about each of their careers.Reid Wiseman, Commander: Wiseman is a Navy veteran and pilot who went to space for the first time in 2014. During that mission, he conducted two spacewalks, totaling 13 hours outside the spacecraft. He recently served as Chief of NASA’s Astronaut Office.Victor Glover, Pilot: Glover is a Navy pilot who took his first trip to space in 2020 as mission pilot of the SpaceX Crew-1 mission to the International Space Station, during which he conducted four spacewalks. Glover will be the first person of color to go to the Moon. Christina Koch, Mission Specialist: Koch worked as an electrical engineer at NASA Goddard and Johns Hopkins Applied Physics Laboratory before becoming a NASA astronaut. She launched on an 11-month mission in 2019, during which she conducted six spacewalks. Koch will be the first woman to go to the Moon.Jeremy Hansen, Mission Specialist: Hansen was a fighter pilot in the Royal Canadian Air Force before becoming a Canadian Space Agency astronaut. Artemis II will be his first spaceflight. Hansen will be the first Canadian to go to the Moon. The Artemis II crew (left to right: Jeremy Hansen, Victor Glover, Christina Koch, Reid Wiseman) pose with Apollo 11 command module Columbia during a visit to the National Air and Space Museum in 2023. Want to learn more about the crew? Check out this blog and keep an eye out for blogs about each of the astronauts in the coming weeks.How Does Artemis II Compare to Apollo? When comparing the Artemis program with NASA’s first Moon landing program, Apollo, the closest analog for Artemis II is Apollo 8. Much like Artemis II, Apollo was the first mission in the Apollo program (and in fact, ever) to send humans to the Moon. One major difference between Apollo 8 and Artemis II, however, is that while Apollo 8 orbited the Moon, Artemis II will fly around the Moon but not enter lunar orbit.Artemis II is anticipated to break Apollo 13’s record (248,655 miles) for the farthest distance humans have traveled from Earth. The Artemis II Orion spacecraft will travel over 4,000 miles beyond the Moon as it flies a loop around the Moon’s far side.What Kind of Spacecraft Carries the Crew of Artemis II? An Orion spacecraft will transport the Artemis II astronauts to the vicinity of the Moon and back. It is capsule-shaped like the Mercury, Gemini, and Apollo spacecrafts and seats four astronauts.Orion has been in development for over 20 years, originally part of an earlier lunar exploration program called Constellation. The crew of Artemis II named their Orion spacecraft Integrity to honor the trust among the people who are bringing this mission to life. The name is also a reference to the massive effort to integrate more than 300,000 individual components into a single spacecraft by teams working across the world.What Kind of Rocket Launches Artemis II?The Artemis II mission will launch using the SLS (Space Launch System) rocket. SLS, which launched for the first time in November 2022 during the Artemis I mission, can launch astronauts aboard the Orion spacecraft and cargo into space. This heavy-lift launch vehicle was developed using a combination of components from the Space Shuttle program (specifically solid rocket boosters and RS-25 engines) and new technology. The Artemis II mission will fly a figure-eight pattern that will take the crew around the Earth and then around the Moon. Watch an animation of the flight path. The Artemis II mission patch, held here by the mission's crew, emphasizes that NASA and its partners are "All In" on the mission. The red line that helps form the A and swirls around the Moon and Earth is representative of the mission’s flight path, which includes the spacecraft flying around both our home planet and its only natural satellite.
