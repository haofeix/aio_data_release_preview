When comparing the Artemis program with NASA’s first Moon landing program, Apollo, the closest analog for Artemis II is Apollo 8. SLS, which launched for the first time in November 2022 during the Artemis I mission, can launch astronauts aboard the Orion spacecraft and cargo into space. This heavy-lift launch vehicle was developed using a combination of components from the Space Shuttle program (specifically solid rocket boosters and RS-25 engines) and new technology. The Artemis II rocket waits on the launch pad. SLS is one of the most powerful rockets to ever launch, providing 8.8 million pounds of thrust (that’s 15% more thrust than the Saturn V Moon rocket). It is powered by two solid rocket boosters (white) and a core stage (orange) with four RS-25 engines. Two minutes after launch, the solid rocket boosters will separate from the core stage and fall back to Earth, splashing into the ocean. (While the Space Shuttle’s solid rocket boosters were reusable, SLS’s are not, and NASA will not be recovering these solid rocket boosters from the ocean.) The Artemis II mission will fly a figure-eight pattern that will take the crew around the Earth and then around the Moon. Watch an animation of the flight path. This will include an opportunity for the crew to pilot the spacecraft in manual mode.Flight Day 2: Orion’s main engine will conduct a trans-lunar injection burn, which sets Orion on its path toward the Moon. This provides a safeguard should Orion experience engine failure.Flight Days 3-5: Orion and its crew continue on their journey towards the Moon, entering the "lunar sphere of influence" on Day 5.Flight Day 6: Orion spacecraft Integrity performs a lunar flyby and conducts both its closest approach to the Moon and its furthest distance from Earth. During the lunar flyby, the crew will make observations and take photos of the far side of the Moon.Flight Day 7-9: Orion spacecraft leaves the “lunar sphere of influence” and begins its journey back to Earth, which includes the first two return trajectory correction burns.Flight Day 10: Orion performs its final return trajectory correction burn and the crew prepares for reentry, conducting the reentry checklist. The Orion crew module and service module separate. The Orion spacecraft reenters Earth’s atmosphere and splashes down in the Pacific Ocean with the help of three main parachutes. A U.S. Navy ship will recover Integrity and the crew, welcoming them back to Earth.Does Artemis II Have a Mission Patch? Like the great space missions that came before it, Artemis II has an official mission patch that will be worn by the crew on the sleeves of their Orion Crew Survival System spacesuits. The patch designates the mission AII, using the roman numeral for 2 to also evoke the visual of the word “All.” It’s a common refrain amongst the crew and other mission support staff at NASA that this mission is “an endeavor of discovery that seeks to explore for all and by all." The Artemis II mission patch, held here by the mission's crew, emphasizes that NASA and its partners are "All In" on the mission. The red line that helps form the A and swirls around the Moon and Earth is representative of the mission’s flight path, which includes the spacecraft flying around both our home planet and its only natural satellite.
