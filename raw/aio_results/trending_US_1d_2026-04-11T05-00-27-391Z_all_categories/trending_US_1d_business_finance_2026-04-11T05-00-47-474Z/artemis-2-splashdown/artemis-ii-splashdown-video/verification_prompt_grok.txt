The answer depends on how you booked your ticket.How airline tickets are structured When booking an economy flight — not first class or extra legroom options — passengers are typically presented with multiple fare options, often labeled "Basic Economy" and "Standard," though names vary by airline.For example, United Airlines offers Basic Economy and United Economy Standard, while American Airlines offers Basic Economy and Main Cabin. The higher-priced option generally includes perks such as: Advance seat selectionEarlier boardingThe ability to earn frequent flyer milesThe cheaper basic option typically does not include seat selection. Passengers who choose these fares are often assigned a seat unless they pay an additional fee to select one in advance.When you are owed a refundUnder U.S. Department of Transportation rules, airlines are required to issue refunds in certain situations.One is a downgrade. If a passenger pays for a higher class, like first class, but is moved to a lower class, like economy, and still takes the flight, the airline must refund the difference in fare. Another applies to optional add-ons, known as ancillary services. These are services not included in the base ticket price, such as paying a separate fee to select a specific seat. So, let's go back to that Standard vs. Basic ticket.The catch with bundled seat selection If a passenger books a standard or main cabin fare where seat selection is included in the ticket price, that feature is considered part of the bundle — not a separate purchase.That means if a passenger selects a window seat but is later moved to a middle seat, federal rules generally do not require the airline to issue a refund. That's because:The seat selection was not purchased as a separate feeThe passenger remains in the same cabin, so it is not considered a downgradeHowever, if a passenger books a lower-cost basic ticket and pays an additional fee to select that same seat, they may be entitled to a refund if the airline does not provide it.
