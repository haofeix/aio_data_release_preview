Episode",[48][49] which were respectively directed by Jennifer Morrison and Pippa Bianco. The production was given a pilot order on March 13, 2018,[50] and on July 30, it was announced that HBO had given the production a series order.[47] The series was renewed for a second season on July 11, 2019.[51] Out of respect for the actors and extras involved, filming of nudity was conducted on a closed set, and for sex scenes, an intimacy coordinator was used.[52][53][54] Production for the second season was scheduled to start in the second quarter of 2020, with the first table read on March 11,[55] but the COVID-19 pandemic delayed the production.[56][57] Production resumed in March 2021, with filming from April to November.[58] HBO defended the series against allegations of a toxic work environment during the production of the second season, writing: "The well-being of cast and crew on our productions is always a top priority. The production was in full compliance with all safety guidelines and guild protocols. It's not uncommon for drama series to have complex shoots, and COVID protocols add an additional layer. We maintain an open line of communication with all the guilds, including SAG-AFTRA. There were never any formal inquiries raised."[59] Before the series' second season, HBO ordered two specials. The first, "Trouble Don't Last Always", premiered on December 6, 2020, and follows Rue as she deals with the aftermath of leaving Jules at the train station and relapsing.[60] The second, "Fuck Anyone Who's Not a Sea Blob", premiered on January 24, 2021, and follows Jules's side of the story.[61] It was co-written and executive produced by Levinson and Hunter Schafer.[62] HBO announced that the special episodes would air two days early on HBO Max.[63] On February 4, 2022, HBO renewed the series for a third season.[64] In September 2022, HBO's CEO Casey Bloys said the series would not end after the third season.[65] Production of the third season was set to start in February 2023,[66] aiming for a late 2023 release,[67] but according to a Vogue interview with Apatow, filming was set to start in the second half of 2023.[68] On a podcast, series costume designer Heidi Bivens said that preparations would begin in May 2023, with filming starting in June 2023.[69] The third season production was disrupted by the 2023 Writers Guild of America strike,[70] with Jeremy O. Harris calling out to David Zaslav, saying, "He's not a scab. David Zaslav, make a deal. That's what I'll say about Season 3 of Euphoria. Make a deal, David. It's easy. Just come to that table."[71] In May 2023, Euphoria was confirmed to take place in the same universe as Levinson's 2023 television series The Idol.[72][73] Euphoria series regular Alexa Demie makes an uncredited cameo appearance in the series' first episode.[74][75] Pre-production for the third season had begun by December 2023.[76][77] On March 12, 2024, Sydney Sweeney said in an MTV interview with Josh Horowitz that filming for the third season was due to "start soon".[78] But on March 25, it was reported that shooting was postponed indefinitely amid speculation that the series would not return. On July 12, 2024, HBO announced that cast members had signed on for a third season that would aim for a January 2025 start date.[79][80] In August 2024, while appearing on an episode of Call Her Daddy with Alex Cooper, Schafer said, "The real tea is I have no fucking idea what's going on" with production on the series' third season.[81] Zendaya said she did not "really have much of an answer" for the future of Euphoria other than it was set to start filming in January 2025, but Domingo said that Levinson had "told me some of it, and it's going to be groundbreaking."[82][83] In June 2018, it was announced that the pilot would star Zendaya, Storm Reid, Maude Apatow, Astro, Eric Dane, Angus Cloud, Alexa Demie, Jacob Elordi, Barbie Ferreira, Nika King, Hunter Schafer, and Sydney Sweeney.[84] In October, Algee Smith was cast to replace Astro as McKay, and Austin Abrams had also been cast.[85] Astro reportedly quit the series after shooting the pilot as he was uncomfortable with the sexual content involving his character.[86] In an interview with GQ, Myha'la confirmed that she auditioned for the lead role that eventually went to Zendaya.[87] In April 2020, Kelvin Harrison Jr. joined the cast, but by May 2021, he had dropped out due to scheduling conflicts as a result of the COVID-19 pandemic.[88][89] In August, Dominic Fike, Minka Kelly, and Demetrius "Lil Meech" Flenory Jr. were added to the cast.[90] In an interview with The Hollywood Reporter, Odessa A'zion confirmed that she auditioned for a role in the second season but after production was halted due to the COVID-19 pandemic, the role went to another actor.[91] On August 24, 2022, Ferreira announced via Instagram story that she had decided to leave the series.[6] On April 5, 2023, she stated: "I just felt like, maybe it's like I overstayed my welcome a little bit. Grant High School in Los Angeles stands in for the fictional East Highland High School.[98] The exterior of the Bennett family house that appears in the pilot episode is located at 5611 Shenandoah Ave, Los Angeles, CA 90056, USA[99] According to the California Film Commission, the first season of Euphoria received $8,378,000 in incentive tax credits. The first season was filmed over 104 days; the second season's production costs totaled $96,685,000 after 176 filming days. The second season received a $19,406,000 tax credit for employing over 15,000 people in California.[100] Zendaya received $500,000 per episode in the first two seasons and $1,000,000 per episode in the third season.[101][102][103] Zendaya said she wanted to explore characters outside the high school setting in the third season,[104] with filming locations in Dublin, London, New York City, Rome, Singapore, and Warner Bros. Studios lots in Burbank and Leavesden.[105] Filming privately began in late January 2025[106] and publicly in February 2025 with Zendaya on set.[107][108][109] Zendaya and Priscilla Delgado were spotted filming a scene from episode 2 on February 28.[110] By March 2, Demie was shooting scenes with Adewale Akinnuoye-Agbaje.[111] Domingo filmed some of his scenes in March and then returned to film the rest of his scenes in July.[112] Elordi and Dane started shooting in April 2025.[113][114][115] In October, both Sweeney and Elordi had wrapped shooting their respective roles.[116] Filming for the season wrapped by early November.[117] As a result of her busy schedule, Zendaya was forced to shoot all her scenes in a truncated timeline stating: "It was a whirlwind. I did what I do in eight months in like four months. It was like trying to get eight episodes in at once. It just flew by me."[118] The show employs hyper-stylized cinematography, set design, costume design, and editing, which presents an "emotional realism" that captures the inner perspectives of the series's adolescent characters.[119][120][121] The first season was shot digitally using the Arri Alexa 65 camera.[122] The two special episodes and second season were shot on Kodak 35mm film stock, primarily Ektachrome, which cinematographer Marcell Rév attributed to a desire to invoke "some sort of memory of high school".[123] The third season was shot on Kodak 35mm and 65mm film, including a new stock called Verita created specifically for the show.[124][125] During pre-production, Levinson reportedly told photographer Petra Collins that he had "written a show inspired by her work and asked her to come and direct". After working with Levinson on the series for five months, Collins was informed by HBO that she was "too young" to direct.[126] Reactions to the series following its release included comparisons to Collins' work and its influence on the show's visual style.[127][128] Use of color and lighting[edit] The series often uses saturated colors, notably purple and blue, to illustrate its characters' emotional states. Shades of green and yellow regularly symbolize distress, while purple and blue convey an elated, feverish atmosphere.[121] In shooting both day and night exteriors, cinematographer Marcell Rév relied on an exaggerated orange-blue color scheme, translated in the use of backlights and tungsten lights, to create visuals that feel "almost dreamlike". LED lights and SkyPanels were used in several interior shoots to display bright and vivid purples and blues.[129] The second season was shot on film, specifically Kodak's Ektachrome and Vision3 500T, which distorted how set lighting looked on camera.[129][130] "For camera movements, we really wanted it to have a certain energy that ties the different storylines together. So, I would say the camera movement is the glue in the show, that glues it together", said Rév of using an energized design element that stands out.[119] Extensive whip pans and tracking shots were employed to portray intimacy, growth, and interrelationship. In the fourth episode of season 1, "Shook Ones Pt. II", a two-minute tracking shot, achieved with a dolly, a technocrane, and four camera stitches, was "to introduce a space where all characters were present, and somehow connect them in one shot".[119][129] The costume design is arguably the series' most notable and influential hallmark.[131][132] The characters often wear chic, flamboyant outfits that serve as "plot devices and psychological profiles" to represent their personalities and character arcs. For example, in season 1, costume designer Heidi Bivens dressed Jules, a transgender girl, in a wardrobe of bright pastels and tennis skirts, inspired by the character's interest in anime and fantasy, to embody "the youthful optimism that comes with a fresh start" and her journey to "conquer" femininity, but as Jules explores her gender identity and becomes more disillusioned between seasons 1 and 2, she "slips into a slightly muted, darker and more androgynous" wardrobe.[133] Labrinth composed original music for the series. Euphoria's score was composed by English singer, songwriter, and record producer Labrinth.[134] The song "All for Us", performed by Labrinth and Zendaya, is hinted at throughout season 1 before being performed as a large musical number at the end of the season finale.[135] Labrinth makes an appearance in the series alongside Zendaya to perform their song "I'm Tired".[136] When you look back to your teenage days, it feels semi-magical but semi-crazy and semi-psychotic. I wanted to make sure the music felt like those things. The series also makes extensive[137] use of popular music, including hip hop, trap, R&B, experimental, indie rock, standards and doo-wop, with some episodes featuring over 20 songs.[138][139] For their work on Euphoria's first season, music supervisors Jen Malone (who also supervises the FX series Atlanta) and Adam Leber won the 2020 Guild of Music Supervisors Award for Best Music Supervision in a Television Drama.[140] In July 2025, it was announced that Hans Zimmer had joined the series to compose the third season.[141] In March 2026, Labrinth released a strongly worded personal statement on his social media where he criticized both HBO and the record label Columbia Records.[142] The following month, Labrinth elaborated on his departure from Euphoria and confirmed that his music had been removed from the third season.[143] The score album for the first season was released by Sony Masterworks through Milan Records on October 4, 2019, for digital download.[144] The album was also released on vinyl on January 10, 2020.[145] The score has been described as "the holy lilt of gospel, orchestral and electronic" and was favorably reviewed by Variety.[146] The score album for the second season was released by Columbia Records on April 22, 2022, in digital and physical formats. Euphoria's first season was met with a positive response from critics, with praise for its acting (in particular of Zendaya), storyline, visuals, and approach to mature subject matter. However, it met with controversy for the amount of drug use and nudity throughout the show. On the review aggregator website Rotten Tomatoes, the first season has an approval rating of 80%, based on 100 critical reviews.[169] The site's critical consensus summary states, "a uniquely challenging and illuminating series, held together by a powerfully understated performance from Zendaya".[169] The review aggregator website Metacritic, which uses a weighted average, assigned a score of 68 out of 100, based on 26 critics.[170] Ben Travers of IndieWire praised the show's authenticity, how HBO "grounds itself in stark reality", and Zendaya's performance and narration.[175] Tim Goodman of The Hollywood Reporter noted Zendaya's performance and the handling of the subject matter.[176] Pilot Viruet of Observer called the show "visually stunning" and praised the ensemble's performance, but criticized the writing as "shaky, filled with clunky lines", and recommended that the show "keep its focus narrow".[177] Jamila Stewart of Vogue stated that Euphoria still has a palpable impact on where fashion trends fall today.[178] The first of the series's two special episodes, "Trouble Don't Last Always", received widespread critical acclaim for its writing, performances, and shift in tone and content from the first season. On Rotten Tomatoes, the episode has a score of 97%, based on 30 critical reviews. The website's critical consensus reads, "Euphoria slows down the tempo without losing the beat in a special episode that pairs a raw Zendaya with a steady Colman Domingo to create small screen magic."[179] On Metacritic, the episode has a weighted average score of 84 out of 100, based on 10 reviews.[180] The second of the two special episodes, "Fuck Anyone Who's Not a Sea Blob", also received critical acclaim, with particular praise for Schafer's performance and writing, as well as the episode's distinctive directorial approach, emotional resonance, and exploration of trans identity. On Rotten Tomatoes, it has a score of 96%, based on 23 critical reviews. The site's critical consensus reads, "By centering on Jules' journey, Fuck Anyone Who's Not a Sea Blob adds welcome depth to her character and gives Hunter Schafer plenty of room to shine."[181] On Metacritic, the episode has an average weighted score of 78 out of 100, based on 10 reviews.[182] The series's second season received mostly positive reviews, with critics praising the performances and visuals but criticizing the pace and characterization. On Rotten Tomatoes, the second season received a score of 78%, based on 113 reviews. The site's critical consensus reads, "As willfully provocative as ever in its second season, Euphoria still isn't for all tastes—but when its addictive ingredients are mixed just right, the results remain intoxicating."[171] Metacritic assigned the season a score of 74 out of 100 based on 19 reviews.[172] IndieWire's Ben Travers criticized the sexual content but appreciated Zendaya's performance, writing, "After seven of the eight episodes, Season 2 is exactly what a drama seeking to spark conversation fears most: It's skippable."[183] Rebecca Nicholson for The Guardian gave the second season two out of five, writing, "this long-awaited second season has decided to lean into its crueller instincts".[184] USA Today's Patrick Ryan praised the performances of Zendaya, Schafer, and Fike, but wrote that "the new episodes are much less captivating when they shift their focus away from Rue and Jules".[185] On the review aggregator website Rotten Tomatoes, 41% of 44 critics' reviews are positive. The website's consensus reads: "Euphoria returns with less than the sum of its parts in a disjointed cavalcade of forced narratives that leave its talented cast stranded in the wind."[173] The review aggregator website Metacritic, which uses a weighted average, assigned a score of 56 out of 100, based on 26 critics.[174] The series' premiere averaged 577,000 viewers in its time slot, a number that increased to one million after the same-night linear replay and preliminary viewing on HBO Go/Now.[186] The hashtag #EuphoriaHBO trended number one in the US and number three worldwide on Twitter after the premiere.[186] The first season was the most watched of HBO's series in the 18–49 demographic[187] with episodes averaging 6.6 million viewers.[188] Season 2 premiere drew 2.4 million viewers across all HBO platforms, a series high. It also marked the strongest digital premiere night performance for any episode of an HBO series since HBO Max's launch,[189] until it was dethroned by House of the Dragon.[190][191] At the end of its second season, it became the second-most-watched HBO series since 2004 (behind Game of Thrones), with episodes averaging 16.3 million viewers[188] until it was surpassed by House of the Dragon.[192]
