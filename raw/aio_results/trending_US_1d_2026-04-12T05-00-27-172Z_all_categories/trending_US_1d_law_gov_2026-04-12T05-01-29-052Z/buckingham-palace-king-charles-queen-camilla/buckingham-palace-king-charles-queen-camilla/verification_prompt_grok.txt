Discover the highlights of a visit to Buckingham Palace, the official London residence of His Majesty The King. Visiting with children? Explore our top highlights for children and families.The State RoomsThe State Rooms are the public rooms in the Palace where the monarch and members of the Royal Family receive and entertain their guests on State, ceremonial and official occasions. They mainly reflect the taste of George IV, who commissioned the architect John Nash to transform Buckingham House into a grand palace in 1825. The State Rooms are furnished with many of the greatest treasures from the Royal Collection, including paintings by Van Dyck and Canaletto, sculpture by Canova, Sèvres porcelain, and some of the finest English and French furniture in the world.Many of the State Rooms have served distinctive purposes both in the past and today. The Throne Room is used for court ceremonies and official entertaining, and was the setting for the wedding photos of The Prince and Princess of Wales. The magnificent Blue Drawing Room was originally used as a ballroom until the 1850s, when new rooms were created for Queen Victoria.The Throne RoomThe Throne Room's dramatic arch and canopy over the thrones was the masterpiece of the architect John Nash, and was greatly influenced by his background in theatre set designs.Central to the room is the pair of throne chairs which are known as Chairs of Estate. They were made for the coronation ceremony of Queen Elizabeth II and Prince Philip in 1953 and were also used for the Coronation of King Charles III and Queen Camilla. You can also see throne chairs made for the coronation of King George V and Queen Mary in 1911, and a single throne chair made for Queen Victoria in 1837.State Portraits of King Charles III and Queen CamillaThe new State Portraits of The King and Queen are now in their permanent home of the Throne Room at Buckingham Palace.State Portraits Hung in Buckingham Palace | Discover the history of State Portraiture Copyright: Royal Collection Enterprises LimitedThe BallroomThis enormous room, the largest of the State Rooms, was completed in 1855, during the reign of Queen Victoria.
