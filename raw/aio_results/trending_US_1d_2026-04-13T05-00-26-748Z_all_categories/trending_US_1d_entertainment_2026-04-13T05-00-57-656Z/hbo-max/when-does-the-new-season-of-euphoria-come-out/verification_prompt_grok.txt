with CDs releasing on May 13, 2022, and vinyl on July 29, 2022.[151][152][153] The album's release was preceded by seven singles, "Watercolor Eyes" by Lana Del Rey, "How Long" by Tove Lo, "(Pick Me Up) Euphoria" by James Blake featuring Labrinth, "Sad4Whattt" by EricDoa, "Yeh I Fuckin' Did it" by Labrinth, "I'm Tired" by Labrinth and Zendaya, and "Elliot's Song" by Dominic Fike and Zendaya.[154][155][156][157][158] In an interview with IndieWire, Labrinth stated of the soundtrack's religious undertones: "We spoke about using organs because of a lot of the religious influences in the show, especially with Rue. We wanted a lot of the sounds edging towards a religious sound. And because I love both Pentecostal and Catholic sounds, I kind of was like trying to merge them both together."[147] Many of the episode titles for season one are references to late-1990s and early-2000s song titles that correlate to the episode itself. For instance, "'03 Bonnie and Clyde" is a reference to the 2002 Jay-Z and Beyoncé song of the same name. The loyal relationship between Nate Jacobs and Maddy Perez in the episode mirrors that between Jay-Z and Beyoncé in the song.[160][14] The series premiered on June 16, 2019, on HBO. In Southeast Asia, Hong Kong, and Taiwan, it premiered on June 17, 2019, through HBO Asia.[161] In Australia, it premiered on June 17, 2019, through Foxtel.[162] In the United Kingdom and the Republic of Ireland, it premiered on August 6, 2019, through Sky Atlantic.[163] The specials were released on December 6, 2020[d] (as "Part 1: Rue") and January 24, 2021[e] (as "Part 2: Jules"). The second season premiered on January 9, 2022.[164] The third season premiered on April 12, 2026, with remaining episodes to be released weekly until the season finale on May 31.[165] The third season premiere also screened at Coachella 2026.[166] [167] The first and second seasons (including the two special episodes) were released on DVD on November 1, 2022, by Warner Bros. Home Entertainment.[168] This is the first A24 series not to be released from Lionsgate Home Entertainment.[168] A Blu-ray and 4K Ultra HD Blu-ray are not yet announced.[168] Zendaya's performance as Rue Bennett received critical acclaim, winning two Emmys for Lead Actress in a Drama Series. Euphoria's first season was met with a positive response from critics, with praise for its acting (in particular of Zendaya), storyline, visuals, and approach to mature subject matter. However, it met with controversy for the amount of drug use and nudity throughout the show. On the review aggregator website Rotten Tomatoes, the first season has an approval rating of 80%, based on 100 critical reviews.[169] The site's critical consensus summary states, "a uniquely challenging and illuminating series, held together by a powerfully understated performance from Zendaya".[169] The review aggregator website Metacritic, which uses a weighted average, assigned a score of 68 out of 100, based on 26 critics.[170] Ben Travers of IndieWire praised the show's authenticity, how HBO "grounds itself in stark reality", and Zendaya's performance and narration.[175] Tim Goodman of The Hollywood Reporter noted Zendaya's performance and the handling of the subject matter.[176] Pilot Viruet of Observer called the show "visually stunning" and praised the ensemble's performance, but criticized the writing as "shaky, filled with clunky lines", and recommended that the show "keep its focus narrow".[177] Jamila Stewart of Vogue stated that Euphoria still has a palpable impact on where fashion trends fall today.[178] The first of the series's two special episodes, "Trouble Don't Last Always", received widespread critical acclaim for its writing, performances, and shift in tone and content from the first season. On Rotten Tomatoes, the episode has a score of 97%, based on 30 critical reviews. The website's critical consensus reads, "Euphoria slows down the tempo without losing the beat in a special episode that pairs a raw Zendaya with a steady Colman Domingo to create small screen magic."[179] On Metacritic, the episode has a weighted average score of 84 out of 100, based on 10 reviews.[180] The second of the two special episodes, "Fuck Anyone Who's Not a Sea Blob", also received critical acclaim, with particular praise for Schafer's performance and writing, as well as the episode's distinctive directorial approach, emotional resonance, and exploration of trans identity. On Rotten Tomatoes, it has a score of 96%, based on 23 critical reviews. The site's critical consensus reads, "By centering on Jules' journey, Fuck Anyone Who's Not a Sea Blob adds welcome depth to her character and gives Hunter Schafer plenty of room to shine."[181] On Metacritic, the episode has an average weighted score of 78 out of 100, based on 10 reviews.[182] The series's second season received mostly positive reviews, with critics praising the performances and visuals but criticizing the pace and characterization. On Rotten Tomatoes, the second season received a score of 78%, based on 113 reviews. The site's critical consensus reads, "As willfully provocative as ever in its second season, Euphoria still isn't for all tastes—but when its addictive ingredients are mixed just right, the results remain intoxicating."[171] Metacritic assigned the season a score of 74 out of 100 based on 19 reviews.[172] IndieWire's Ben Travers criticized the sexual content but appreciated Zendaya's performance, writing, "After seven of the eight episodes, Season 2 is exactly what a drama seeking to spark conversation fears most: It's skippable."[183] Rebecca Nicholson for The Guardian gave the second season two out of five, writing, "this long-awaited second season has decided to lean into its crueller instincts".[184] USA Today's Patrick Ryan praised the performances of Zendaya, Schafer, and Fike, but wrote that "the new episodes are much less captivating when they shift their focus away from Rue and Jules".[185] On the review aggregator website Rotten Tomatoes, 41% of 44 critics' reviews are positive. The website's consensus reads: "Euphoria returns with less than the sum of its parts in a disjointed cavalcade of forced narratives that leave its talented cast stranded in the wind."[173] The review aggregator website Metacritic, which uses a weighted average, assigned a score of 56 out of 100, based on 26 critics.[174] The series' premiere averaged 577,000 viewers in its time slot, a number that increased to one million after the same-night linear replay and preliminary viewing on HBO Go/Now.[186] The hashtag #EuphoriaHBO trended number one in the US and number three worldwide on Twitter after the premiere.[186] The first season was the most watched of HBO's series in the 18–49 demographic[187] with episodes averaging 6.6 million viewers.[188] Season 2 premiere drew 2.4 million viewers across all HBO platforms, a series high. It also marked the strongest digital premiere night performance for any episode of an HBO series since HBO Max's launch,[189] until it was dethroned by House of the Dragon.[190][191] At the end of its second season, it became the second-most-watched HBO series since 2004 (behind Game of Thrones), with episodes averaging 16.3 million viewers[188] until it was surpassed by House of the Dragon.[192] According to Variety, Euphoria became the most tweeted television series of the decade in the US, with more than 30 million tweets related to the series during the second season, 51% more than during mature content[edit] Some commentators and organizations have criticized the series's explicit content, including self-harm, excessive drug use, and sexual material amongst its teenage characters,[202] content present in other HBO series, including Big Little Lies, Game of Thrones, Girls, Luck, and Westworld.[203] The conservative media advocacy group Parents Television and Media Council called the series "dark, depraved, degenerate and nihilistic", and asked HBO and AT&T to end it.[204] Common Sense Media, which provides information on media's suitability for children, also noted the strong adult themes and advised against teenage viewership.[205] One scene involving more than 30 shots of penises was criticized by both critics and supporters, with Esquire calling it "pointlessly gratuitous".[206] The Guardian wrote that writers and producers should find new and different ways to shock audiences.[207] In 2022, Minka Kelly said she felt discomfort at the quantity of nude scenes in the series.[208] Drug Abuse Resistance Education criticized the series's depiction of drug use, saying that it "chooses to misguidedly glorify and erroneously depict high school student drug use addiction ... and other destructive behaviors as common in today's world".[209] Samuel Getachew wrote in a Culture piece for Vogue that the series' depictions of trauma aestheticize it in a way that his "generation is particularly vulnerable to".[210] In 2023, Colman Domingo said that he felt the accusations by other series actors of a toxic workplace due to lengthy shoots and the alleged mistreatment of actors, as well as onset chaos during the second season, are overblown.[211] Levinson acknowledged the controversies over the series's content, saying that some parents will be "totally freaked out".[212] Augustine Frizzell, who directed the pilot episode, said that the explicit content should help foster a conversation between parents and teenagers.[213] Levinson also said that he hopes the series "opens up a dialogue" due to the "disconnect between parents and teenagers".[39] Zendaya issued a warning both before the series and season 2 premiere about its "deeply emotional subject matter".[214] HBO voiced objections to some sexually graphic scenes, but said it would not interfere with the series' "creative process".[212] The series includes viewer discretion warnings and a website for mental health and other support group resources.[215][216] The series has reportedly been censored for sexual or violent content in countries like Malaysia,[217]
