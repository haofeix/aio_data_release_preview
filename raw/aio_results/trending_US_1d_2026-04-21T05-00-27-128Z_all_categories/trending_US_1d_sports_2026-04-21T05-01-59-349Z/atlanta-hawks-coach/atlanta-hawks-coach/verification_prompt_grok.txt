Quin Snyder - Head Coach
Quin Snyder was named the 15th full-time head coach of the Atlanta Hawks on
February 26, 2023.

Atlanta finished the 2024-25 season at 40-42, a +4-win increase from the 2023-24
season. The Hawks ranked second in the NBA in assists per game (29.6, most in
franchise history) and steals per game (9.73), third in points in the paint per game
(55.0), opponent turnovers per game (16.0) and free throws made per game
(18.0).

Under the tutelage of Snyder and his staff, Dyson Daniels earned the Kia NBA Most
Improved Player Award and was runner-up for the NBA Defensive Player of the
Year. Zaccharie Risacher was named to the Kia NBA All-Rookie First Team while
finishing as runner-up for NBA Rookie of the Year.

Snyder owns 458 career regular season wins, the fifth-most among all active head
coaches. Snyder won his 450th career game on Mar. 12, 2025, as Atlanta topped
Charlotte at State Farm Arena, 123-110.

Snyder served as head coach of the Utah Jazz from the 2014-15 to 2021-22
seasons, accumulating a 372-264 record (.585) and leading the Jazz to the playoffs
in six of his eight seasons. Over his final six seasons (2016-17 to 2021-22), the Jazz
compiled a 294-178 record, the third-highest winning percentage (.623) in the
NBA and best in the Western Conference over that span. Utah started the 2020-21
season with a 23-5 record, earning Snyder the honor of being named the head
coach of the 2021 Western Conference All-Star team, and finished the regular
season with the best record in the NBA (52-20; .722) and the fifth-highest single-
season winning percentage in franchise history. His 372 wins are the second-most
in Jazz history behind only Jerry Sloan.

Snyder finished Top 10 in NBA Coach of the Year voting in four-of-five seasons
between 2017-21, including second in 2018, third in 2021, sixth in 2017 and
eighth in 2019. He is a four-time Western Conference Coach of the Month.
The Jazz reached the playoffs in six consecutive seasons, advancing to the Western
Conference Semifinals on three occasions (2017, 2018 and 2021).

Before his hiring by the Jazz in 2014-15, Snyder was an assistant coach with the
Hawks during the 2013-14 season. Prior to joining the Hawks in 2013-14, Snyder
worked as an assistant coach for CKSA Moscow (2012-13), the Los Angeles Lakers
(2011-12) and Philadelphia 76ers (2010-11). From 2007-10, Snyder was head
coach of the Austin Toros (the NBA G League affiliate of the San Antonio Spurs),
recording a 94-56 (.627) mark, while leading the Toros to three straight playoff
appearances and the 2008 National Basketball Development League
championship game. He was the 2008-09 D-League Coach of the Year.
Snyder began his head coaching career at the University of Missouri in 1999,
where in seven seasons, he led the Tigers to a 128-96 (.571) record, and four
NCAA Tournaments including an Elite Eight appearance. Prior to starting with
Missouri, he served as an assistant with the Los Angeles Clippers (1992-93) under
Larry Brown and at Duke (1995-99) under Mike Krzyzewski.

Snyder was named a McDonald’s All-American at Mercer Island High School. He
played in 136 games over four years (1985-89) at Duke University, reaching three
Final Fours and earning Academic All-American honors.

Snyder earned undergraduate degrees in philosophy and political science from
Duke, later earning a Juris Doctor and Master’s in Business Administration (MBA)
from Duke as well. He is a native of Mercer Island, WA.

 Bryan Bailey - Assistant Coach
Bryan Bailey is in his third season as an assistant coach with the Hawks. He came
to Atlanta after spending four years as an assistant coach with the Utah Jazz.

Previously, Bailey served as an assistant coach with the Salt Lake City Stars and the
Westchester Knicks, both of the NBA G League.
Bailey most recently served as head coach of the Hawks’ 2025 summer league
team, leading the club to a 4-1 mark, Atlanta’s best summer league record since
beginning play in Las Vegas in 2010, and tying for the third-best record at this
year’s Las Vegas Summer League.

The Hempstead, N.Y. native’s 13-year playing career took him to Germany
(Wurzburg, Artland, Oldenburg, Medi Bayreuth), the Netherlands (Landstede
Zwolle), Bosnia (KK Bosna), Poland (PGE Turow), Cyprus (Keravnos) and Italy
(Virtus Roma).

He is the co-founder of DIME Athletics, which focuses on assisting competitive
high school, collegiate and pro basketball players on improving their basketball
skills, strength, conditioning and overall athleticism.

Bailey played collegiately at Bucknell University, where he was named First Team
All-Patriot League in his final two seasons. He is a Certified Strength and
Conditioning Specialist (CSCS) by the National Strength and Conditioning
Association.

 Mike Brey - Assistant Coach
Mike Brey is in his third season as an assistant coach with the Hawks following a
memorable run as head coach at Notre Dame, where he finished as the
winningest coach in school history, accumulating a 483-280 (.633) mark over 23
seasons.
Under his guidance, the Irish reached the postseason 18 times, including 13 NCAA
Tournament berths, three Sweet 16 trips, two Elite Eight appearances and the
2015 ACC Championship.
Originally named head coach at Notre Dame in 2000, Brey was named the
Associated Press and the Henry Iba/USBWA National Coach of the Year award in
2011, while also being named BIG EAST Coach of the Year in 2006-07, 2007-08 and
2010-11.
Prior to his arrival at Notre Dame, Brey served as head coach at University of
Delaware, where he compiled a 99-52 mark from 1995-2000 and won America
East Co-Coach of the Year honors in 1998.
Brey was previously an assistant coach at Duke University for eight years, helping
the Blue Devils to advance to the Final Four six times and win back-to-back
national championships in 1991 and 1992.
Before entering the collegiate ranks, Brey played for and coached with legendary
coach Morgan Wootten and DeMatha High School.
He is a graduate of George Washington University with a degree in physical
education. Brey and his wife, Suzanne, have four children between them - Kyle,
Callie, Emmett and Wade – and Brey has three grandchildren, Olivia, Jude and
Wes.

 Steve Klei - Assistant Coach
In his third full season in Atlanta is assistant coach Steve Klei. He also serves as
head coach of the Hawks’ NBA G League affiliate, the College Park Skyhawks, a
position he was named to in September, 2024.

Last season, Klei led the Skyhawks to a 26-24 record, including a 9-7 Showcase Cup
record and 17-17 regular season mark. Under Klei’s lead, the Skyhawks finished in
the top-10 in multiple defensive categories for both seasons combined, while also
breaking single season franchise records for most three pointers (668), most
assists (1211), and most rebounds (2337).

Klei and his staff were instrumental in the development of Mouhamed Gueye,
who spent 24 games with the Skyhawks on assignment before ending the year in
the rotation for the Hawks, where he appeared in 33 games (28 starts). The
Skyhawks also saw four players receive NBA callups in Dominick Barlow, Tony
Bradley, Kevon Harris and Daeqwon Plowden.

Klei joined the Hawks in March, 2023, after spending the first part of the 2022-23
season with Olimpia Milano in Italy as director of player development.

He was previously with the Utah Jazz from 2016-22, spending time in the DAV
program (player development/advanced scouting/video), including as head video
coordinator prior to becoming a coaching associate.

He began his career with the Canton Charge of the NBA G League in 2015-16. Klei
is a graduate of Indiana University with a degree in Kinesiology, Sports Marketing
and Management.

 Antonio Lang - Assistant Coach
In his third season as a Hawks assistant coach is Antonio Lang.

He came to Atlanta after four seasons with the Cleveland Cavaliers as an assistant
coach following five seasons with the Utah Jazz (2014-19).

Before joining the Jazz, Lang served as head coach of the Mitsubishi Diamond
Dolphins in Japan for four years.
Selected by Phoenix with the 29th overall pick in the 1994 NBA Draft, Lang played
in 143 career regular season games with the Suns, Cavaliers, Miami Heat, Toronto
Raptors and Philadelphia 76ers over six seasons.

He also played in the Continental Basketball Association (CBA), International
Basketball League (IBL), in the Philippines and in Japan. He is fluent in Japanese.
Raised in Mobile, Al., Lang played four collegiate seasons at Duke University
(1990-94), appearing in three Final Fours and winning back-to-back national titles
in 1991 and 1992.

 Sanjay Lumpkin – Assistant Coach
Former College Park Skyhawks player Sanjay Lumpkin is in his third season as an
assistant coach with the Hawks.

Lumpkin came to Atlanta from the Utah Jazz, where he was a player development
coach in 2022-23 following two years on Utah’s DAV (player
development/advanced scouting/video) staff.

He played five collegiate seasons at Northwestern University (2012-17) before
playing in the NBA G League with the Erie BayHawks (2018-19) and the Skyhawks
in 2019-20.

Lumpkin holds a degree in learning & organizational change from Northwestern,
where he also earned a Master’s in Sports Administration.
 Ronald Nored - Assistant Coach
Ronald Nored is in his third season as an assistant coach with the Hawks. He came
to Atlanta after spending the prior two seasons on the Indiana Pacers’ coaching
staff following three years with the Charlotte Hornets.

The Hawks’ summer league head coach in 2024, Nored held the same position
previously with Indiana (2022) and Charlotte (2019).
The former head coach of the Long Island Nets of the NBA G League, Nored also
spent time with the Maine Red Claws. He began his coaching career at
Brownsburg High School near Indianapolis.

Nored played collegiately at Butler University, where he helped lead the Bulldogs
to back-to-back National Championship game appearances in 2010 and 2011. He
was a two-time Horizon League Defensive Player of the Year.

Nored and his wife Danielle have four children; daughters Avery and Campbell,
and sons Kai and Jude.

 Reggis Onwukamuche - Assistant Coach
Former College Park Skyhawks player Reggis Onwukamuche is in his third season
with the Hawks and second as an assistant coach. He spent 2023-24 as a player
development coach prior to his promotion.

Onwukamuche came to Atlanta after having worked with the Utah Jazz in DAV
(player development/advanced scouting/video) during the 2021-22 season and
with the Milwaukee Bucks in 2022-23 as assistant video coordinator/player
development.

As a player, the native of Houston, Texas appeared in 144 career games in the NBA
G League, also spending time with the Lakeland Magic and Erie BayHawks. He was
also part of the Hawks’ mini-camp roster in September 2020.

Onwukamuche played collegiately at Prairie View A&M from 2013-15, appearing
in 64 contests.

 Ryan Schmidt - Assistant Coach
Ryan Schmidt is in his third season as an assistant coach with the Hawks.

In 2023-24, he also served as head coach of the College Park Skyhawks of the NBA
G League, where he guided the team to the best defensive season in franchise
history. The Skyhawks led the G League in opponent points per game (102.9), field
goals made per game (35.7) and points in the paint (44.0), while ranking second in
defensive rating (105.5) and opponent field goal percentage (.444).

Schmidt came to Atlanta after spending the 2022-23 campaign as head coach of
the London Lions of the British Basketball League, where he was named the 2023
BBL Coach of the Year after leading the team to three domestic titles.

Schmidt previously served as head coach of the Brampton Honey Badgers of the
Canadian Elite Basketball League from 2020-22, where he earned 2022 CEBL
Coach of the Year honors for leading the Honey Badgers to the CEBL
Championship. He was also an assistant coach for Raptors 905 of the NBA G
League for four seasons (2017-21).
A native of Keizer, Ore., Schmidt played college basketball at the University of
Hawaii (2001-04) and at Western Oregon University (2004-05). While at Western
Oregon, Schmidt served as team captain and led the Wolves in scoring his senior
year before being selected by Idaho in the 2008 NBA Development League Draft.
Schmidt played for Honolulu in the American Basketball Association during the
2009-10 season.

He and his wife, Whitney, have a daughter named Deklynn and a son, Beckham.

 Ekpe Udoh – Assistant Coach
Longtime NBA and international big man Ekpe Udoh is in his third season as an
assistant coach with the Hawks after 13 professional playing seasons.

A former sixth overall pick in 2010 by Golden State, Udoh appeared in 384 regular
season games (56 starts) for the Warriors, Milwaukee Bucks, LA Clippers and Utah
Jazz, with international stops in Israel, Turkey, China, Italy and Japan.

With Fenerbahce (Turkey) from 2015-17, Udoh earned All-Euro League First Team
honors and Final Four MVP, leading the Euro League in rebounding and blocks en
route to the championship, playing alongside former Hawk Bogdan Bogdanovic.

Udoh played collegiately at Michigan (2006-08) and Baylor (2009-10). He is an avid
reader who created “Ekpe’s Book Club” during his playing career, routinely hosting
club meetings and social media chats.
 Ashton Smith - Assistant Coach
Ashton Smith is in his second season with the Hawks and first as an assistant
coach. He spent the 2024-25 campaign as a player development coach after time
in the 2023-24 season with the College Park Skyhawks as an assistant coach.
Smith has previous coaching experience with the London Lions, Raptors 905 and
Hamilton Honey Badgers of the CEBL.

His playing career spanned eight years and five countries, including Austria,
Canada, Colombia, Indonesia and the NBA G League.

Smith played collegiately at Indiana University of Pennsylvania (IUP), where he left
as the all-time leader in wins and assists, was a three-time conference champion,
the 2011-12 conference player of the year, and 2011-12 NCAA Division II All-
American. He will be inducted into the school’s Hall of Fame later this year.

Born and raised in Scarborough, Ontario, Canada, Smith’s parents are from
Jamaica and Barbados, respectively. Smith and his wife, Falan, have a son, Kairo
and a daughter, Kalina.

 Jeff Watkinson - Assistant Coach
Jeff Watkinson is in his third full season as an assistant coach for the Hawks. He
returned to the organization in March, 2023, after spending seven seasons as
assistant coach/integrated player development with the Utah Jazz. Watkinson was
originally with the Hawks as the team’s strength and conditioning coach from
2012-15.
Prior to his initial stint with the Hawks, Watkinson served in similar capacities at
Indiana University, University of Missouri, Clemson University, UNC Charlotte and
the College of Charleston.

The suburban Chicago native is a graduate of North Carolina State University (pre-
medicine), where he played collegiate baseball. He later earned a Master’s in
Exercise Science from Indiana University.

Jeff is the father of two children – daughter Evelyn (7) and son Dax (5).

Early life and education[edit] Brey, the son of Olympic swimmer Betty Brey,[9] graduated from DeMatha Catholic High School in Hyattsville, Maryland in 1977. As a two-year letter winner under coach Morgan Wootten, Brey helped the team to a 55–9 mark. He enrolled at Northwestern State University, where he played varsity basketball for three years (1977–1980). He played one season at George Washington in 1981–82 after sitting out the 1980–81 season as a transfer. He served as team captain and was named most valuable player with 5.0 points and 4.8 assists per game for the Colonials.[10] In 1982 Brey graduated from George Washington University with a bachelor's degree in physical education. Brey returned to his former high school, becoming an assistant coach under Morgan Wootten. In 1987, he was hired by Duke University to assist Mike Krzyzewski, and in 1995 he took over his first head coaching job at the University of Delaware. Brey guided the Fightin' Blue Hens to a 99–51 record over five years, leading the team to two America East Conference Championships and subsequently two trips to the NCAA Tournament. In 2000, Brey became the head coach at the University of Notre Dame. Notre Dame Fighting Irish[edit] In 2000, Brey succeeded Matt Doherty as head coach of the Notre Dame Fighting Irish men's basketball team. Notre Dame had not been to the NCAA tournament since 1990. Brey led the Irish to the NCAA tournament in his first three years as head coach (2001–2003), notching a Sweet Sixteen appearance in 2003. He has since led the team to tournament appearances in 2007, 2008, 2010, 2011, 2012, 2013, 2015, 2016, 2017 and 2021. On December 29, 2017, Brey tied Digger Phelps for most wins by a Notre Dame coach with 393. During the 2007–08 season, Brey led the Irish to a 24–6 regular-season mark. He was named the Big East Coach of the Year for the second consecutive season on March 11, 2008.[11] Notre Dame had a 45-game home winning streak between February 2006 and February 2009 – the second-longest in school history. By completing the 2007–2008 regular season 18–0 at home, Brey coached the first team in Big East history to have consecutive undefeated seasons at home. On June 19, 2012, Brey signed a 10-year extension to remain the head coach of the Notre Dame Irish up until 2022. The financials were not released. During the 2014–15 season, Brey's Notre Dame team went 32–6 and won the ACC conference tournament. The squad advanced to the Elite Eight, losing a close game to Kentucky. The 32 wins were the most by a Notre Dame men's team since 1908–09. He also passed Hall of Famer George Keogan for second place on Notre Dame's all-time wins list, trailing only Digger Phelps. Notre Dame advanced to the Elite Eight for the second consecutive season, defeating Michigan, Stephen F. Austin, and Wisconsin as the 6 seed in the East region. Notre Dame lost to North Carolina 88–74 in the Elite Eight. During the 2016–17 regular season, Brey's team went 23–8. They finished the season in a three-way tie with Florida State and Louisville. The Irish were given a 3-seed in the ACC tournament which guaranteed them a double bye. Notre Dame dominated its first two games against Virginia and Florida State and sparked another ACC tournament final appearance for the second time in three years. The Irish went on to lose in the tournament final to Duke, 75–69. Notre Dame received a 5-seed in the West Region of the NCAA tournament, and defeated Princeton in the first round before falling to West Virginia in the second round. Resignation from Notre Dame[edit] With his 2022–23 team struggling to stay out of the basement of the ACC standings, on January 19, 2023, Brey announced that the 2022–23 season would be his last as head coach at Notre Dame, although he said that he was definitely not done coaching.[12][13] On June 15, 2023, Brey was hired as assistant coach for the Atlanta Hawks.[14] He reunited with head coach Quin Snyder, who played his junior and senior seasons at Duke while Brey was an assistant under Mike Krzyzewski. The two then coached at Duke together during the 1994–1995 season. Quin Snyder - Head Coach
Quin Snyder was named the 15th full-time head coach of the Atlanta Hawks on
February 26, 2023.

Atlanta finished the 2024-25 season at 40-42, a +4-win increase from the 2023-24
season. The Hawks ranked second in the NBA in assists per game (29.6, most in
franchise history) and steals per game (9.73), third in points in the paint per game
(55.0), opponent turnovers per game (16.0) and free throws made per game
(18.0).

Under the tutelage of Snyder and his staff, Dyson Daniels earned the Kia NBA
Most Improved Player Award and was runner-up for the NBA Defensive Player of
the Year. Zaccharie Risacher was named to the Kia NBA All-Rookie First Team while
finishing as runner-up for NBA Rookie of the Year.

Snyder owns 458 career regular season wins, the fifth-most among all active head
coaches. Snyder won his 450th career game on Mar. 12, 2025, as Atlanta topped
Charlotte at State Farm Arena, 123-110.

Snyder served as head coach of the Utah Jazz from the 2014-15 to 2021-22
seasons, accumulating a 372-264 record (.585) and leading the Jazz to the playoffs
in six of his eight seasons. Over his final six seasons (2016-17 to 2021-22), the Jazz
compiled a 294-178 record, the third-highest winning percentage (.623) in the
NBA and best in the Western Conference over that span. Utah started the 2020-21
season with a 23-5 record, earning Snyder the honor of being named the head
coach of the 2021 Western Conference All-Star team, and finished the regular
season with the best record in the NBA (52-20; .722) and the fifth-highest single-
season winning percentage in franchise history. His 372 wins are the second-most
in Jazz history behind only Jerry Sloan.

Snyder finished Top 10 in NBA Coach of the Year voting in four-of-five seasons
between 2017-21, including second in 2018, third in 2021, sixth in 2017 and
eighth in 2019. He is a four-time Western Conference Coach of the Month.
The Jazz reached the playoffs in six consecutive seasons, advancing to the Western
Conference Semifinals on three occasions (2017, 2018 and 2021).

Before his hiring by the Jazz in 2014-15, Snyder was an assistant coach with the
Hawks during the 2013-14 season. Prior to joining the Hawks in 2013-14, Snyder
worked as an assistant coach for CKSA Moscow (2012-13), the Los Angeles Lakers
(2011-12) and Philadelphia 76ers (2010-11). From 2007-10, Snyder was head
coach of the Austin Toros (the NBA G League affiliate of the San Antonio Spurs),
recording a 94-56 (.627) mark, while leading the Toros to three straight playoff
appearances and the 2008 National Basketball Development League
championship game. He was the 2008-09 D-League Coach of the Year.
Snyder began his head coaching career at the University of Missouri in 1999,
where in seven seasons, he led the Tigers to a 128-96 (.571) record, and four
NCAA Tournaments including an Elite Eight appearance. Prior to starting with
Missouri, he served as an assistant with the Los Angeles Clippers (1992-93) under
Larry Brown and at Duke (1995-99) under Mike Krzyzewski.

Snyder was named a McDonald’s All-American at Mercer Island High School. He
played in 136 games over four years (1985-89) at Duke University, reaching three
Final Fours and earning Academic All-American honors.

Snyder earned undergraduate degrees in philosophy and political science from
Duke, later earning a Juris Doctor and Master’s in Business Administration (MBA)
from Duke as well. He is a native of Mercer Island, WA.

 Mike Brey - Assistant Coach
Mike Brey is in his third season as an assistant coach with the Hawks following a
memorable run as head coach at Notre Dame, where he finished as the
winningest coach in school history, accumulating a 483-280 (.633) mark over 23
seasons.

Tournament berths, three Sweet 16 trips, two Elite Eight appearances and the
2015 ACC Championship.
Originally named head coach at Notre Dame in 2000, Brey was named the
Associated Press and the Henry Iba/USBWA National Coach of the Year award in
2011, while also being named BIG EAST Coach of the Year in 2006-07, 2007-08 and
2010-11.

Delaware, where he compiled a 99-52 mark from 1995-2000 and won America
East Co-Coach of the Year honors in 1998.
Brey was previously an assistant coach at Duke University for eight years, helping
the Blue Devils to advance to the Final Four six times and win back-to-back
national championships in 1991 and 1992.
Before entering the collegiate ranks, Brey played for and coached with legendary
coach Morgan Wootten and DeMatha High School.
He is a graduate of George Washington University with a degree in physical
education. Brey and his wife, Suzanne, have four children between them - Kyle,

Wes.
