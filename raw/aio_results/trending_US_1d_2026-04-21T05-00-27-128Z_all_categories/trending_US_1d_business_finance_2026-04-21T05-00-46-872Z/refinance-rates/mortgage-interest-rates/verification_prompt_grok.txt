Should you refinance your mortgage? Whether you should refinance your mortgage right now depends on a few factors: Can you get a significantly lower rate? Refinancing to a lower rate will lower your monthly payment and help you save money over the loan term. Are you shifting from a variable rate to a fixed rate? If you have an adjustable-rate mortgage (ARM) that’s resetting soon, refinancing to a fixed-rate loan would provide a more predictable payment for your budget going forward. Do you want to change your term? Many people also adjust the length of their mortgage when they refinance. Typically this means shortening the term from 30 years to 15 years to pay off the mortgage faster, or lengthening the term up to 30 years to reduce the monthly payments. Do you want to tap your equity? Many people choose a cash-out refinance to turn their home equity into cash by borrowing against it. Non-conforming loans do not meet Fannie and Freddie’s requirements — jumbo loans, which are for amounts higher than the conforming limit, are a common example. Because they carry more risk for lenders, jumbo loans typically have stricter requirements and may come with higher rates. Fixed-rate loans vs. adjustable-rate loans Fixed-rate mortgages lock in your interest rate for the life of the loan, offering the benefit of predictable monthly payments that are easier to budget around. In contrast, adjustable-rate mortgages typically start with a lower introductory rate, then adjust periodically based on market conditions. This means your rate, and your payments, could rise or fall at various intervals over time.
